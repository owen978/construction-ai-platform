## Overview
AI helps site managers most with the daily paperwork: diaries and daily reports, toolbox talks, inductions, method statements and checklists. You type or dictate your notes at the end of the day, and it turns them into a clear record in your company's format.

The value is getting the record written while it's fresh, because a good diary is what you rely on months later when a delay or defect is argued about. What it can't do is see the site. It doesn't know the ground conditions, who turned up or whether the scaffold tag is in date. You do.

Start with one task, such as the daily report. Use a business account with data controls, not a personal one, and read every draft against what actually happened before you sign it or brief it out. AI drafts and organises. Safety decisions stay with you and the principal contractor.

## Where AI helps most
- Daily reports and diaries: turns rough notes or voice memos into a record of weather, labour, plant, deliveries, progress, instructions and issues.
- Toolbox talks: drafts a short talk on the topic you choose, with questions to check people understood.
- Method statements and inductions: drafts the structure for you and the specialist contractor to check against the real job.
- Quality and snagging checklists: produces an inspection checklist for a trade, or a room-by-room snagging template.
- Subcontractor coordination: lays out a weekly schedule of who's working where, with access, plant and hold points.
- Weather and waste: drafts a weather delay record from your diary, and a site waste management plan.

## Where to be careful
- RAMS and method statements: hazards and controls must come from the people doing the work. The site manager, for the principal contractor, decides whether work starts, and temporary works go through the temporary works coordinator.
- Records you'll rely on later: diaries and daily reports are often the best evidence of what happened. Keep them factual, don't let the tool fill gaps, and keep your original notes.
- Weather and delay: whether bad weather is a relevant event, and what notice you need to give, depends on the contract. Check your contract and take advice.
- Personal data: diaries often include names, injuries and photos of people. Keep them out of personal AI accounts.

## FAQs
### Can AI write a RAMS?
It can draft one, and it's a quick way to get the structure and obvious hazards down. But a RAMS must reflect the actual task, site, people and plant. The contractor doing the work owns it, the site team reviews it before work starts, and an AI tool can't be the competent person.

### How do site managers use AI?
Mostly for writing: daily reports, toolbox talks, inductions, method statement drafts, checklists and emails. Some record voice notes on the walk round and let the tool write them up. The judgement stays with the site manager.

### Can AI write a daily site report?
Yes. Give it your notes on weather, labour, plant, deliveries, work done and issues, and it will produce a consistent report. Check numbers and names before you file it, because these records can be used as evidence later.

### Will AI replace site managers?
Not the job itself. Running a site means walking the job, managing people, spotting what's unsafe and making calls when things change. AI can take some of the evening admin off you, but it can't take responsibility for the site.

### Is it safe to use AI for site safety documents?
To draft them, yes, if a competent person checks everything. The danger is a generic document that looks complete but doesn't match the site. Never treat AI output as a temporary works design, a permit to work or a specialist's assessment.
