## Overview
AI helps construction directors most with turning a lot of project information into a clear picture for decisions: board reports, executive summaries, portfolio reviews and risk. The workflow on this page creates an executive project summary from your project data, with a red, amber or green status, the programme and commercial position, the key risk and the decision needed from the board.

At director level, the bigger question is how the business uses AI. That means deciding which tasks to start with, which tool the company approves, what data can go into it and who checks the output. AI can't set strategy, judge people or carry accountability for a project. It can make the information you rely on quicker to produce and easier to read.

Start small. Pick one recurring report, such as the monthly board summary, and one approved business account with data controls. Write a short policy on what staff can and can't put into AI tools, and make sure every output is checked against the source reports.

## Where AI helps most
- Executive summaries: AI condenses a project's programme, commercial and risk position into a short summary for the board.
- Portfolio reviews: it puts monthly reports from several projects into the same format, so you can compare them quickly.
- Risk reporting: it turns long risk registers and commercial risk summaries into the few points leaders need to act on.
- Bid decisions: it applies your bid/no-bid criteria consistently, so pursuit decisions are recorded and comparable.
- Lessons learned: it pulls close-out and lessons learned reports into themes you can feed back into tendering and delivery.
- AI policy and rollout: it helps draft a simple company AI policy and a plan for which teams and tasks to start with.

## Where to be careful
- Accountability. Board decisions and statutory duties, including under CDM 2015 and the Building Safety Act, stay with people. Reports drafted with AI must be checked by whoever owns the figures.
- Softened bad news. Summaries can smooth over a problem. Ask for risks to be stated plainly, with an owner and an action.
- Data and policy. Without an approved tool, staff will use personal accounts with company data. Provide a business account with data controls and say clearly what's allowed.
- Claims and disputes. A summary of a claim or dispute can overstate or understate the position. Check your contract and take advice before relying on it.

## FAQs
### How can construction companies use AI?
Mostly for the written and repetitive work: reports, minutes, correspondence, method statements, quote comparisons and contract summaries. The best results come from picking specific recurring tasks, giving staff tested prompts and checking the output. It works alongside your existing systems, not instead of them.

### Should a construction company have an AI policy?
Yes. A short policy should cover which tools are approved, what data can and can't go into them, who checks outputs and how mistakes are reported. Without one, people tend to use personal accounts, which puts client and company data at risk.

### Where should a construction business start with AI?
With one or two recurring tasks that take real time and are easy to check, such as monthly reports or meeting minutes. Choose one business tool with data controls, build prompts for those tasks and review how it's going before rolling it out further.

### Is AI worth it for a small construction company?
Often, because small firms carry the same paperwork as large ones with fewer people to do it. Business AI plans are usually paid per user, so you can start with a few people and one or two tasks. Whether it pays depends on choosing tasks that recur and judging the results honestly.
