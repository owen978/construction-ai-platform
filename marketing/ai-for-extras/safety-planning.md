## Overview
AI helps with safety planning by drafting the first version of method statements, risk assessments, COSHH assessments, toolbox talks and the construction phase plan from the details you give it about the site and the task. The competent person then starts from a structured draft, not a blank page or the last job's copy with the names changed.

It can't walk the site, spot the hazard nobody mentioned or judge whether a control will work in practice. Under CDM 2015 the principal contractor plans, manages and monitors the construction phase, and HSE guidance expects the paperwork to fit the job and the level of risk. AI drafts and organises. A competent person checks it, changes it and signs it off.

Start with one document, such as next week's toolbox talk. Use a business account with data controls switched on, not a personal one, and treat everything it writes as a draft until a competent person has reviewed it.

## Where AI helps most
- Method statements: the sequence of work, plant, access, permits and controls set out step by step from your description of the task.
- Risk assessments: hazards, who could be harmed and the controls laid out consistently, with prompts for hazards you may have missed.
- COSHH assessments: hazards, exposure routes, controls, PPE and emergency steps, built from the safety data sheet you supply.
- Toolbox talks: a short, plain-language talk tied to this week's work, with a few questions to check people understood.
- Construction phase plans: sections such as the management structure, site rules, welfare and emergency arrangements, from your project information.
- Plain English: rewriting dense documents so the people doing the work can follow them.

## Where to be careful
- Sign-off: AI takes no responsibility. People do. Never put a name on a RAMS or plan that the competent person hasn't read and changed to suit the site.
- Generic content: AI writes plausible, generic controls. Check every control is really in place on site, such as the edge protection, the exclusion zone or the dust extraction.
- High-risk work: lifting operations, temporary works, confined spaces, asbestos and work at height need specialist input, permits and appointed people. A draft is no substitute.
- Accuracy and data: AI can quote out-of-date rules or the wrong PPE. Check it against HSE guidance and the safety data sheet, and keep accident and health details out of personal AI accounts.

## FAQs
### What is the difference between a risk assessment and a method statement?
A risk assessment identifies the hazards, who could be harmed and the controls needed. A method statement sets out how the job will be done safely, step by step, with those controls built in. Together they're usually called RAMS.

### Who is responsible for the construction phase plan?
Under CDM 2015 the principal contractor draws it up where there's more than one contractor, and the contractor does it where there's only one. The client must make sure a plan is in place before the construction phase starts. It should be kept up to date as the work changes.

### Who should write a RAMS?
Usually the contractor doing the work, because they know the method, with input from the people doing the job. The principal contractor reviews it against the construction phase plan before work starts. AI can draft it, but a competent person checks it and the team gets briefed on it. A RAMS nobody has read is just paper.

### What is a COSHH assessment in construction?
An assessment of the risks from hazardous substances such as cement, silica dust, solvents and resins, and how exposure will be controlled. Start with the safety data sheet, look at how people could be exposed, and work through other controls before relying on PPE. Check it against HSE's COSHH guidance.
