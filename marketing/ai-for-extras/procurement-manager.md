## Overview
AI helps procurement managers most with the documents and checks around buying: prequalification questionnaires, supplier evaluations, scopes for subcontract packages and procurement schedules. The workflow on this page drafts a pre-qualification questionnaire covering company details, financial standing, experience, resources, health and safety, quality and environmental credentials.

AI is useful for getting a sensible first draft down, comparing returns side by side and pulling out the exclusions, qualifications and missing information that cause trouble later. It can't judge whether a supplier is financially sound or competent for the work, and it doesn't know your supply chain. That judgement stays with you and your team.

Start with one task you repeat on every package, such as comparing quotes against the scope. Use a business AI account with data controls, never a personal one, because supplier prices and accounts are confidential. Check every comparison against the original returns before you recommend anyone or place an order.

## Where AI helps most
- Prequalification: AI drafts a PQQ in proportion to the package, using areas such as those in PAS 91, so you only ask for what you will actually check.
- Quote comparisons: it lays several quotes side by side and lists exclusions, qualifications and gaps against your scope.
- Scopes of works: it drafts package scopes with inclusions, exclusions, attendances and interfaces, so every bidder prices the same thing.
- Procurement schedules: it sets out lead times, order dates and delivery milestones from the programme, so long-lead items are ordered in time.
- Supplier evaluation: it summarises PQQ returns, insurances and accreditations into a consistent scoring sheet for your team to mark.
- Enquiry letters: it drafts tender enquiries and clarification questions that say clearly what you need and by when.

## Where to be careful
- Supplier judgement. Financial standing, competence and references need checking by a person. A tidy summary of a PQQ return isn't due diligence.
- Competence questions. The Building Safety Act and CDM 2015 both put weight on competence. Make sure your questions fit the work, especially on higher-risk buildings, and take advice if you're unsure.
- Public sector rules. If you're buying for a public body, the Procurement Act 2023 sets the process. AI can help draft documents, but the process, criteria and scoring must follow the Act and what you've published.
- Confidential pricing. Supplier quotes and accounts should only go into a business account your company has approved, and never be shared between bidders.

## FAQs
### How is AI used in construction procurement?
Mostly for drafting and checking: PQQs, scopes of works, enquiry letters, quote comparisons and procurement schedules. It speeds up the paperwork and makes comparisons more consistent. Decisions on who to appoint, and on what terms, stay with your team.

### Can AI compare subcontractor quotes?
Yes, and it's one of the most useful jobs for it. In a business account, give it your scope and the quotes and ask for every exclusion, qualification and assumption in a table. Then check the table against the quotes, because it can still miss a line in a long document.

### Can AI write a PQQ?
It can draft a good one quickly, and PAS 91 is a sensible reference for the areas to cover. Keep it proportionate, because a long questionnaire for a small package often gets no reply. If you're buying for a public body, the Procurement Act 2023 sets the process, so follow it rather than a generic template.

### Can AI check a supplier's financial standing?
It can help you read accounts, summarise the key figures and list questions to ask. It can't confirm a firm is sound, and published accounts can be out of date. Use credit checks, references and your own judgement alongside it.
