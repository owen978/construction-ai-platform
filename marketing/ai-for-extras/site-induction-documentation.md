## Overview
AI helps with site induction documentation by turning your construction phase plan, site rules and logistics plan into a clear induction briefing, with shorter versions for visitors and delivery drivers and a checklist for the person giving it. It can also turn the briefing into slides with speaker notes.

It can't decide what the hazards are or keep up with the site on its own. The induction has to match this site this week: the access routes, the assembly point, the crane, the live services. Under CDM 2015 the principal contractor makes sure a suitable induction is given, and the site manager owns the content. AI drafts. The site team checks it, delivers it and keeps the record.

Start with one job, such as updating your induction for the next phase of work. Use a business account with data controls switched on, not a personal one, and walk the content against the site before you use it.

## Where AI helps most
- Induction briefings: site rules, emergency procedures, PPE, welfare, key contacts and this site's hazards, drafted from your own documents.
- Visitor and delivery briefings: a short version covering the rules, escort arrangements, the traffic route and the unloading area.
- Checklists and records: a checklist for the person giving the induction, questions to check understanding and a sign-off sheet showing the version and date.
- Phase changes: a list of what's changed between the old induction and your updated plan.
- Slides and notes: a short presentation with speaker notes and space for the site plan.
- Plain English: simpler wording, plus a draft translation for a fluent speaker to check.

## Where to be careful
- Site-specific content: a generic induction is easy to produce and easy to ignore. Check every hazard, route, contact and assembly point against the site, not last job's version.
- Competence checks: an induction doesn't prove anyone is trained or fit for the work. Card checks, training records and task briefings stay with the site team.
- Translations: AI can get safety terms wrong or lose a warning. Have a fluent speaker check it, and use pictures and the site plan as well as words.
- Data: induction forms hold names, emergency contacts and sometimes health information. That's personal data under UK GDPR, so keep it out of personal AI accounts and store the records securely.

## FAQs
### What should a site induction include?
Who's who, the site rules and PPE, emergency arrangements and first aid, welfare, permits to work, this site's hazards and their controls, and how to report accidents, near misses and concerns. Keep it about this site rather than a general safety talk, and check people have understood it before they start.

### Can a site induction be done online?
Part of it can. Some contractors send general content, such as company rules, out as a video or online module before the first day. The site-specific part, covering the layout, the hazards, the emergency route and who's in charge, is best done on site, in person, with time for questions.

### How do you induct workers who don't speak much English?
Use simple words, pictures, the site plan and demonstrations, and check understanding with questions rather than asking "do you understand?". Use a translated version or an interpreter, such as a supervisor who speaks the language. AI can produce a draft translation, but a fluent speaker should check it before anyone relies on it.

### When should a worker be re-inducted?
When the site changes in a way that affects them, such as a new phase, a new access route, a crane going up or a change to the emergency arrangements. Also when someone comes back after a long gap. Brief everyone on significant changes, not just new starters.
