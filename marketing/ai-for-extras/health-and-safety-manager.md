## Overview
AI helps health and safety managers most with drafting and organising the documents behind a safe site: construction phase plans, COSHH assessments, RAMS reviews, toolbox talks and inspection reports. You give it the project details or a safety data sheet, and it gives you a structured first draft to make specific to the site.

It's good at structure, plain language and making sure the usual headings are covered. It can't see the site, and it doesn't know your hazards, your people or what's changed since last week. It can also sound confident while being wrong.

Start with one document type you write often. Use a business account with data controls, and keep incident details and personal information out of personal accounts. Check every hazard, control and reference against the site, the safety data sheet and HSE guidance. AI drafts and organises. The competent person, the principal contractor and the principal designer decide and sign off.

## Where AI helps most
- Construction phase plans: drafts the structure and standard arrangements, ready for the principal contractor to make site specific.
- COSHH assessments: turns a safety data sheet and how the product is used into a draft with hazards, controls, PPE and emergency measures.
- RAMS reviews: compares a subcontractor's RAMS against your checklist and lists the gaps to raise with them.
- Toolbox talks: turns a method statement, a recent incident or an HSE topic into a short talk with questions for the team.
- Inspection reports: sorts site inspection notes into findings, priorities, owners and close-out dates.
- Incident write-ups: structures the facts of an incident or near miss for the investigation, without drawing the conclusions for you.

## Where to be careful
- Sign-off: AI can't be the competent person. Whether work is safe to proceed is for the principal contractor, the principal designer, the temporary works coordinator and the competent people they appoint.
- Generic content: an assessment that could apply to any site is a warning sign. Check it against the actual product, task, location and people.
- Wrong references: tools can quote out-of-date guidance, wrong exposure limits or regulations that don't exist. Check against current HSE guidance and the safety data sheet.
- Incidents: keep names, injuries and witness statements out of personal accounts. Whether an incident is reportable under RIDDOR is for you to decide from HSE guidance, not the tool. If a claim follows, take advice.

## FAQs
### Can AI write a COSHH assessment?
Yes, as a draft. Give it the safety data sheet and say how the product will be used, where and for how long. A competent person must check it against the real task, including any exposure limits and health surveillance needs in current HSE guidance.

### Can AI write a construction phase plan?
It can draft the structure and standard content, such as site rules, welfare, emergency procedures and the significant risks. Under CDM 2015 the principal contractor must have a plan specific to the project in place before the construction phase starts, and keep it updated.

### Can AI do a risk assessment for construction work?
It can help you write one, but it can't assess the risk. That needs someone who knows the task, the site and the people. Use AI to prompt for hazards you might have missed and to tidy the write-up.

### Will AI replace health and safety managers?
It's very unlikely. The job needs judgement, presence on site and the authority to stop work. AI is more likely to cut the time spent formatting documents, leaving more time for inspections and talking to the workforce.

### How is AI used in construction safety?
Mostly for documents: plans, assessments, toolbox talks and inspection reports. Some sites also use camera systems that flag missing PPE or people near plant. Treat these as an extra pair of eyes, not a replacement for supervision.
