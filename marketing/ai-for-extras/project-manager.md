## Overview
AI helps construction project managers most with the paperwork that comes round every week and every month: progress reports, meeting minutes, risk registers and correspondence. You give it your notes, figures and programme position, and it gives you a structured first draft to check and finish.

It's good at structure and tone. It turns rough notes into minutes with actions and owners, and a month of site updates into a client report. What it can't do is know your project. It doesn't know what was agreed on the phone, which risks are real, or what your contract says unless you give it the document. Every figure, date and name in the output is yours to check.

Start with one task that eats your time, such as the monthly report. Use a business account with data controls, so project information isn't used to train the model, and check what your company allows. Then compare the draft with your notes before anything goes out.

## Where AI helps most
- Progress reports: turns site diaries, cost updates and programme notes into a consistent monthly report.
- Meeting minutes: turns rough notes into minutes with decisions, actions, owners and dates.
- Risk registers: suggests risks for the project type and stage, with causes and mitigation, for the team to score and own.
- Correspondence and change control: drafts emails, letters and change control notices that set out the change and its likely effect on cost and programme.
- Planning documents: drafts a project execution plan summary, stakeholder plan or pre-construction meeting agenda from your scope and team.
- Close-out: pulls a closeout report and lessons learned together from your records.

## Where to be careful
- Notices: AI can draft the letter, but it won't reliably know the notice rules in your contract, especially an amended JCT or NEC4 form. Check your contract and take advice.
- Filled gaps: if your notes don't say it, the tool may invent a plausible date, figure or name. Check every fact before a report reaches the client.
- Risk scoring: a generated register is a prompt list, not a risk assessment. The team decides likelihood, impact and who owns each risk.
- Confidential information: client details, tender figures and commercial positions never go into a personal AI account. Use a business account your company has approved.

## FAQs
### Will AI replace construction project managers?
Unlikely. AI can draft reports, minutes and letters, but it can't chair a difficult progress meeting, settle an argument between trades or take responsibility for a decision. What it will change is how much of the week goes on paperwork.

### How can project managers use AI in construction?
Mostly for documents: progress reports, minutes, risk registers, correspondence, change control and close-out reports. You give it your notes and data, it gives you a structured draft, and you check it against the source.

### Can AI write a construction progress report?
Yes, as a first draft. Give it the month's diaries, programme position, cost summary and key issues, and it will put them into a consistent structure. Progress, forecast dates and costs must come from your records, not the tool.

### Can AI write contractual notices under JCT or NEC4?
It can draft the letter, but don't rely on it to know what your contract requires. Timing, content and how a notice must be served vary between forms and are often amended. Check your contract and take advice.

### Is it safe to use ChatGPT for project documents?
Only with the right account. Personal accounts may use what you type to improve the model unless you change the settings. For client or commercial information, use a business account with data controls and follow your company's policy.
