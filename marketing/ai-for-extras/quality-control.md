## Overview
AI helps with quality control most by drafting the checklists and records that show work was built right: inspection checklists, inspection and test plans, non-conformance reports, snagging lists and handover evidence. You give it the specification, the trade and your site notes, and it gives you a structured document to check.

Quality control depends on checking work at the right time and writing down what you found. AI helps with both: checklists and inspection and test plans before work starts, then NCRs, snag lists and handover records afterwards.

It can't inspect anything. Whether work is acceptable is a judgement against the specification and drawings, made by the site manager, the designer or the client's inspector. Start with one checklist for a trade that causes repeat problems. Use a business account with data controls, check the draft against the actual specification, and make sure the right person signs each inspection off.

## Where AI helps most
- Inspection checklists: builds a checklist for a trade or element from the specification and manufacturer's instructions, with what good looks like.
- Inspection and test plans: drafts an ITP with acceptance criteria, records and hold and witness points, for the team to agree.
- Non-conformance reports: writes up an NCR with the description, specification reference, cause, proposed fix and close-out actions.
- Snagging records: turns walk-round notes into a list grouped by area and trade, with a responsible trade against each item.
- Design reviews: produces a design review checklist so coordination problems get caught before they reach site.
- Handover evidence: checks your handover file against what should be there, and lists what's missing.

## Where to be careful
- Acceptance: AI can't decide whether work is acceptable. That's for the site manager, the designer or the client's inspector, judged against the specification.
- Fire safety: fire stopping, cavity barriers and fire doors need checking by competent people with the right evidence, and higher-risk buildings have stricter requirements under the Building Safety Act. A checklist reminds you to look. It doesn't sign anything off.
- Wrong references: tools can quote clauses, tolerances or standards that aren't in your contract documents. Check against the actual specification.
- Contract consequences: NCRs, defects and handover records can affect completion, payment and retention. Keep them factual. Check your contract and take advice.

## FAQs
### Can AI be used for quality control in construction?
Yes, for the paperwork: checklists, ITPs, NCRs, snagging lists and handover records. The inspection, and the decision on whether work is acceptable, still need a competent person on site.

### What is an ITP in construction?
An inspection and test plan sets out what gets checked, when, against which criteria, by whom and what record is kept. It marks hold points, where work stops until inspected, and witness points, where the client's side can attend. AI can draft one, but the team must agree it before work starts.

### What should a non-conformance report include?
What was found and where, the requirement it fails with the specification or drawing reference, photos, the cause, the proposed fix, who will do it and how it will be closed out. If the fix needs a design change, the designer has to approve it.

### Can AI spot defects in site photos?
Some tools claim to, and image recognition is improving, but it isn't reliable enough to replace an inspection. AI can help describe, sort and label your photos. A person still inspects the work and signs it off.

### What should be in a construction handover pack?
Typically as-built drawings, O&M manuals, test and commissioning certificates, warranties and the health and safety file required under CDM 2015. On higher-risk buildings, golden thread information is handed over too. Check your contract for what the client expects.
