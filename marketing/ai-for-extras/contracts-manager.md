## Overview
AI helps contracts managers most with the contract paperwork that builds up across several jobs at once: delay notices, extension of time assessments, change instructions, subcontract summaries and the papers behind a dispute. Those are the five workflows on this page. Each one turns your facts and records into a structured draft under NEC4 or JCT.

AI is good at getting a first draft down, putting events in order and summarising long documents in plain English. It doesn't know what your contract says unless you give it the contract, amendments included, and it can't decide entitlement. It will also quote clause numbers with confidence when they're wrong.

Start with one task, such as a plain-English summary of a subcontract you're about to sign. Use a business AI account with data controls, never a personal one, and check every clause reference against the signed contract. Where notices, time limits or disputes are involved, check your contract and take advice before anything is sent.

## Where AI helps most
- Delay notices: AI drafts a clear notice from your facts, setting out the event, its cause and its likely effect, ready for you to check against the contract.
- Extension of time assessments: it organises delay events, records and programme evidence into a structured assessment you can test and finish.
- Change instructions: it writes up a change order or project manager's instruction with scope, cost, programme effect and approvals.
- Subcontract summaries: it turns a long subcontract into a plain-English summary of scope, payment, variations, risk and key obligations.
- Dispute summaries: it sets out the background, the issues, each party's position and the evidence, so you and your advisers start from a clear picture.
- Correspondence: it drafts firm, factual letters that stay professional when the relationship is under strain.

## Where to be careful
- Notices and time limits. Form, content, recipient and method of service all depend on your contract, and under NEC4 a late notice can lose you the claim. Check your contract and take advice.
- Clause references. AI will cite clauses from the wrong form or edition. Check every reference against the signed contract and its amendments.
- Entitlement. Whether an event gives time, money or neither is a judgement on the contract and the facts, not something to accept from a draft.
- Disputes and confidentiality. Keep contracts, without prejudice material and legal advice out of personal accounts, and ask your advisers how they want dispute material handled.

## FAQs
### Can AI write a contractual notice?
It can draft one quickly from the facts you give it. The contract decides what the notice must contain, who it goes to, how it's served and when. Check your contract and take advice before sending anything that matters.

### Can ChatGPT review a construction contract?
It can summarise a contract and flag clauses worth a closer look, such as payment, liability, design responsibility and termination. It isn't a legal review, and it can miss or misread amendments. Use it in a business account to prepare for a proper review, not to replace one.

### Can AI help with an extension of time claim?
Yes, with the organising and the writing. It can put delay events in order, link them to your records and draft the narrative. It can't do the delay analysis for you or decide entitlement. Check your contract and take advice.

### Can I use AI to prepare for adjudication?
It can help you build the chronology, summarise documents and set out the issues clearly. The Construction Act gives a right to refer disputes to adjudication and the process moves quickly, so get advice early. Check your contract and take advice, and agree with your advisers how AI is used on dispute material.
