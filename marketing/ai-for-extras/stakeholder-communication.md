## Overview
AI helps with stakeholder communication by turning rough notes, long email chains and meeting scribbles into clear letters, minutes and updates that say what's happening, who's doing what and by when. It's useful for client progress emails, letters to neighbours, minutes with an action table, a pre-start meeting agenda, RFI responses and a stakeholder register that maps who needs to hear what, and how often.

It doesn't know the history behind a relationship, what was agreed on site last Tuesday, or which email could end up as evidence in a dispute. It drafts. You decide what goes out and in whose name. Anything that touches delay, instructions or payment needs reading against the contract first.

Start with one job, such as the minutes from your next progress meeting. Use a business account with data controls switched on, not a personal one, and read every draft as the person receiving it would before you send it.

## Where AI helps most
- Meeting minutes: rough notes or a voice memo turned into minutes with decisions, actions, owners and dates.
- Project correspondence: a clear email or letter from your bullet points, with the facts in a sensible order.
- Stakeholder plans: a register of everyone affected, from the client to neighbours and the local authority, with what they care about and how often they hear from you.
- Pre-start meetings: an agenda covering roles, scope, programme, health and safety, information release and payment.
- RFI responses: the query, design intent, resolution and drawing updates laid out for site to act on.
- Neighbour notices: plain-English updates about deliveries, road closures and noisy work.

## Where to be careful
- Contract: an email can become a notice. Early warnings, delay notices, instructions and anything about payment have their own rules on content, timing and who receives them. Check your contract and take advice.
- Tone: AI tends to be wordy and over-polite, or too blunt when asked to push back. Don't send an apology that reads like an admission of liability.
- Accuracy: AI can give an action to the wrong person, smooth over a disagreement or fill gaps with invented dates and drawing numbers. Minutes become the record, so the chair checks them before they go out.
- Data: names, addresses and complaints from neighbours are personal data under UK GDPR. Keep them out of personal AI accounts.

## FAQs
### What should construction meeting minutes include?
The date, who attended, apologies, then each item with what was decided and the actions, each with an owner and a due date. Record decisions and actions, not a transcript. Issue them quickly and ask for corrections by a set date.

### How should you deal with complaints from neighbours on a building site?
Give neighbours a named contact, log every complaint, acknowledge it quickly and tell them what you're doing about it. Warn them before noisy work, deliveries or road closures, rather than after. AI can draft the letters and keep the log tidy, but someone on site has to turn up and sort the problem.

### Can I use AI to write emails to clients?
Yes, as a drafting aid, but you're still responsible for what's sent. Check the facts, dates and tone, and don't let it add promises you haven't made. If the email reads like a contractual notice, check your contract and take advice before it goes.

### What is an RFI in construction?
A request for information: a formal question to the design team about a gap, clash or unclear detail in the drawings or specification. A good response gives a clear answer with drawing references. If the answer changes the scope, it may be a variation that needs a proper instruction, so check your contract.
