## Post 1 — Value / listicle (attach bim-ai-copilot-guide-listicle.png)

"Copilot" is doing a lot of work in the BIM market right now, and it means three completely different things depending on who is selling it.

So here is the honest version of what one can actually do:

→ Answer questions about the data you export
→ Find the RFI or clause you half remember
→ Draft BEPs, TIDPs and coordination reports
→ Group a 3,000-row clash export by root cause
→ Audit file naming and status against ISO 19650
→ Not see your model, not edit your geometry

Everything in that list is text, structure and pattern. Everything it cannot do is geometry or accountability.

The boundary falls in the same place every time. If a product claims to cross it, it is either using a different definition of BIM or overselling.

Get the boundary right and the useful part is not that a decision gets replaced. It is that a diagnosis that took six hours now takes four minutes, so it gets run weekly instead of once a fortnight in a panic.

## Post 2 — Hook / time-saver (attach bim-ai-copilot-guide-hook.png)

"Are we going to hit the Stage 4 data drop, and what is going to stop us?"

Answering that properly by hand is most of a day. Cross-reference the TIDP against the register, find what is stuck at the wrong status, work out which gaps actually block the drop.

Four minutes with a copilot, on a school project last month:

1. Export the drawing and model register from the CDE (612 rows)
2. Paste it alongside the TIDP and the data drop requirement
3. Ask for every container short of the required suitability, grouped by originator, with days sitting at current status
4. Ask separately for anything in the CDE that is on no TIDP line at all

47 containers short. 31 of them one consultant, all last touched the same day five weeks ago, which is a resourcing problem wearing a technical costume.

And 14 containers on no TIDP line at all. A subconsultant had been uploading into the wrong folder structure for a month.

It decided nothing. It just made the diagnosis cheap enough to repeat.

## Post 3 — Short / group version (attach bim-ai-copilot-guide-hook.png)

Quick one for the BIM managers here.

Before you put anything from your CDE into an AI tool, three checks, and they take an afternoon:

What your information protocol actually says about third-party processing. Whether your vendor tier trains on your data (business tiers generally do not, consumer tiers sometimes do). And whether you need to send that much in the first place, because most of these workflows run fine on a redacted register export.

The Building Safety Act point is the one people miss. A copilot can draft a golden thread narrative and check it for gaps. A named dutyholder still signs it.

We wrote up the whole thing free: the three kinds of copilot, what to feed one, and a UK worked example.
