## Post 1: Value / listicle (attach best-ai-construction-software-listicle.png)

Every construction software demo now comes with the words "AI-powered" on the first slide.

Some of it is useful. Some of it is a search box with a new name.

Six questions to ask before you sign anything:

→ What does the AI actually do, and what would a person do without it?
→ What data does it need from us, and in what condition?
→ How accurate is it, and how do I check?
→ Is our project data used to train your models?
→ What is the total cost over three years, including implementation?
→ Can we pilot it on a live project first?

One more test that cuts through most pitches: if the AI feature disappeared tomorrow, would you still want the software?

The good products are solid construction software first. The AI just makes them faster to use.

## Post 2: Hook / time-saver (attach best-ai-construction-software-hook.png)

Most contractors choose AI software in the wrong order.

They start with a product demo. They should start with the bottleneck.

Here is the order that works:

1. Ask each department where the hours are going
2. Match each bottleneck to a category (takeoff, site capture, scheduling, platform)
3. Check you have the data that category needs
4. Run a paid pilot on live work, with a number to hit
5. Do the sums on hours saved against the annual cost
6. Decide what not to buy this year

Worked through for a £28m regional contractor, that points to about £20k across three targeted tools, not a six-figure platform bought on the strength of a demo.

And for anything that is purely reading and writing, a £20 a month general assistant usually does the job.
