## Post 1: Value / listicle (attach ai-for-construction-project-management-listicle.png)

Most construction PMs spend a third of their week writing documents, not managing projects.

That is the part AI is actually good at.

Six jobs worth handing it first:

→ Monthly progress reports, assembled from the site diaries
→ Risk register reviews that flag stalled risks and silent owners
→ 3 and 6 week look-aheads from a programme export
→ Meeting minutes with an owner and date on every action
→ Change log summaries for the monthly report
→ First drafts of early warnings and delay notices

What it should not touch: the critical path, the negotiation, the call on whether to accelerate. Those stay with you.

One rule makes all six safe. Tell it to use only what you give it, and to list the gaps instead of filling them.

## Post 2: Hook / time-saver (attach ai-for-construction-project-management-hook.png)

Six hours. That is how long the monthly report took on a £4.2m school extension.

Twenty site diaries, a programme update, the cost report, the risk register, the accident book. Read it all, rewrite it all, every month.

This month the PM pasted the lot into an AI assistant with one standing instruction: write it in the client's seven headings, use only what is provided, and list anything missing or contradictory at the end.

Draft back in minutes. But the useful bit was the list at the end:

1. Roofer off site for four days, no reason recorded
2. A drainage variation in the diaries that never made the change log
3. The steel frame risk still open, three weeks after topping out

The unlogged variation alone was worth several thousand pounds.

Total time: about 90 minutes. The PM still wrote the programme commentary. That part is judgement.
