## Post 1 — Value / listicle (attach ai-construction-estimating-listicle.png)

A tender lands with 18 drawings, a 90-page spec, preliminaries, a PCI pack and a 12-day return.

The estimator spends the first two and a half days just reading.

That is where AI earns its keep in estimating. Not in setting the price. In everything around it:

→ Tender document review and scope summary
→ Take-off schedule structure and cross-checks
→ NRM2 bill of quantities drafting
→ Subcontract quote comparison
→ Rate build-ups from your own figures
→ Qualifications and clarifications

On a Leeds office fit-out, the document review flagged a missing asbestos survey and an out-of-hours working clause. The quote comparison showed the cheapest M&E price had left out builder's work, commissioning and O&M manuals. Added back, it was second most expensive.

Estimate out in 8 days instead of 11. The estimator still made every pricing decision.

## Post 2 — Hook / time-saver (attach ai-construction-estimating-hook.png)

Ask an AI tool for the rate for 100mm blockwork and it will give you a confident number.

Do not use it.

That number is not from a current price book. It does not know your region, your gang productivity, or this month's material prices. It may be years out of date.

The safe way to use AI in estimating:

1. Give it your labour rates and supplier prices. Never ask it for rates.
2. Let it structure the bill in NRM2. Do the totals in a spreadsheet.
3. Always ask: "List any documents referred to but not included."
4. Check every measurement rule before it leaves your desk.

AI structures. You price. A spreadsheet calculates.

What AI gets wrong, and how to avoid it, is in the guide linked in the comments.
