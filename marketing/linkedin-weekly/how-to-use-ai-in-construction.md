## Post 1 — Value / listicle (attach how-to-use-ai-in-construction-listicle.png)

Most contractors try AI by opening ChatGPT, typing "help me with construction", getting something generic, and concluding the industry is not ready.

The tool was never the problem. The task was.

AI is strong on work that is document heavy, repetitive in shape, and already reviewed by a competent person. That is a very specific list:

→ RAMS and method statements
→ Daily site reports
→ Tender return analysis
→ Meeting minutes and actions
→ Monthly progress reports

Notice what is not on it. Nothing requiring site presence. Nothing requiring measurement. Nothing where AI makes the decision.

A Midlands groundworks firm we spoke to takes most of a day to write a deep drainage RAMS. With the right input and a proper review step, it is about 45 minutes. The document is also more complete, because the hazard list does not depend on how tired the contracts manager is on a Friday afternoon.

The rule that makes all of this work, and keeps you compliant under CDM 2015: AI drafts, a competent person owns.

## Post 2 — Hook / time-saver (attach how-to-use-ai-in-construction-hook.png)

The reason your AI output is generic is not the tool. It is that you gave it a one-line prompt.

Every prompt that actually works in construction has five parts:

1. Role. "You are a UK construction health and safety manager."
2. Task. The exact document you want.
3. Context. The real project detail. Scope, access, plant, sequence, constraints.
4. Format. Your headings, your structure, your house style.
5. Constraints. UK terminology, and this one matters most: "flag anything you need me to confirm, do not invent it."

That last line is the difference between a draft full of invented spec references and a draft that ends with eleven honest questions for you to answer.

Thin input, thin output. Give it the drawing references, the subcontract scope, the site notes and your previous version of the same document, and the first draft lands close.

Then review it properly. Always.

## Post 3 — Short / group version (attach how-to-use-ai-in-construction-hook.png)

Quick one for anyone in construction wondering where to actually start with AI.

Do not start with the tool. Start with the single task that eats the most of your week and produces a document at the end of it. For most people that is RAMS, daily reports, or tender analysis.

Give the AI your real project information, not a vague description. Specify your format. Tell it to flag gaps rather than fill them. Then review every number and reference before it leaves your desk.
