## Post 1: Value / listicle (attach chatgpt-vs-claude-for-construction-listicle.png)

"Should we use ChatGPT or Claude?"

I get asked this more than any other AI question in construction. The honest answer is that it depends on the job, not the person.

Here is how it splits on real site and commercial work:

→ Tender packs: Claude
→ Contract amendments: Claude
→ Subcontract quote comparisons: Claude
→ Voice-note site diaries: ChatGPT
→ Photos of delivery tickets and defects: ChatGPT
→ RAMS drafts: either, checked by a competent person

The pattern is simple. Claude is better at reading long documents and telling you what is missing. ChatGPT is better at fast, varied, on-the-move jobs.

Both are around £18 to £20 a month. For a QS or commercial manager, paying for both often pays back in the first week.

Whichever you pick, use a business plan for client documents and never let either one set your rates.

## Post 2: Hook / time-saver (attach chatgpt-vs-claude-for-construction-hook.png)

Same subcontract pack. Same prompt. Two AI tools.

We modelled a test on a typical package review: a drylining package on a £5.2m school extension. ChatGPT and Claude got the same 64 pages: amendments, scope, spec, programme extract and the sub's tender return.

The prompt: list every commercial risk, with the clause reference, and say clearly if anything is missing.

Both found the retention mismatch (5% vs 3%), the excluded fire stopping and the 14 vs 11 week programme gap.

Claude also caught the pay-less notice period amended to 2 days, and flagged that no acoustic testing was specified.

ChatGPT missed the pay-less notice and invented a design responsibility clause that was not in the documents.

Then the twist: ChatGPT wrote the covering email to the subcontractor far faster.

Claude to read it. ChatGPT to write it up. That split is what most teams I speak to end up with.
