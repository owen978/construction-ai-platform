## Post 1 — Value / listicle (attach ai-in-construction-safety-listicle.png)

Ask any UK health and safety manager where their week goes and the answer is rarely "on site".

It goes on paperwork. RAMS for every activity. Subcontractor RAMS to review. COSHH for every substance. Permits. Toolbox talks. The audit trail that proves all of it happened.

That is the part AI should be doing. Not watching your site.

Where it genuinely helps today:

→ First-draft RAMS in a minute, not an afternoon
→ COSHH assessments straight from the safety data sheet
→ Triage 30 subcontractor RAMS so you know which 3 need real attention
→ Toolbox talks built from your own near misses, so people actually listen
→ Patterns across years of near-miss free text nobody has ever read
→ Hours handed back to the person who should be on site

On one 2.8m refurbishment we looked at, 31 substances were COSHH assessed in 90 minutes. The backlog had been running for months. And AI triage caught a demolition method statement that contradicted the temporary works design, before work started.

Here is the part nobody should skip: AI cannot be the competent person. CDM 2015 puts duties on people, not software. Every draft still needs a named competent person who has read it, corrected it, and owns it.

But it can stop consuming the hours that making those decisions well depends on.

## Post 2 — Hook / time-saver (attach ai-in-construction-safety-hook.png)

A generic toolbox talk on manual handling, delivered from a laminated sheet for the fifth time, achieves nothing. Everyone in the industry knows it.

A talk about the near miss that happened on this site last Thursday, involving this crew, gets attention.

The barrier was never willingness. It was time. Nobody has capacity to write a bespoke talk on Monday about Thursday's incident.

That barrier is gone:

1. Paste in the near-miss detail
2. Ask for a focused 10-minute talk in plain language
3. Get discussion questions and the specific controls you want reinforced
4. Deliver it while people still remember the event

Same principle across the whole safety file. COSHH from the data sheet. First-draft RAMS from the activity and the site conditions. The competent person reviews instead of typing.

The advisor on the project we wrote up doubled their site time. Not because AI made safety decisions, but because it stopped eating the hours before they got to make any.
