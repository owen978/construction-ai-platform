## Post 1 — Value / listicle (attach generative-ai-in-construction-listicle.png)

Most contractors trying AI give it ten minutes, get a generic answer, and decide it is useless.

The problem is almost never the tool. It is that nobody told them which jobs it is actually good at.

Here is where generative AI genuinely earns its keep on a UK project:

→ Drafting RAMS and method statements in your format
→ Reading a 150-page contract and finding the clause in seconds
→ Comparing subcontractor tender returns like for like
→ Writing up variations and valuation narratives
→ Turning rough site notes into a proper daily report
→ Generating early concept options at RIBA Stage 1 to 2

And where it does not work, which matters just as much:

Not arithmetic. Not regulation citations without checking. Not anything safety-critical without a competent person signing it.

One groundworks contractor we looked at took RAMS drafting from 2.5 hours to about 40 minutes, almost all of which is now review. Cost: £100 a month across five users.

The return did not come from the technology being clever. It came from picking one high-volume document and doing it properly.

## Post 2 — Hook / time-saver (attach generative-ai-in-construction-hook.png)

2.5 hours down to 40 minutes per RAMS.

That is what a 40-person Midlands groundworks contractor got out of generative AI. Not from a platform rollout. From five £20 licences and one afternoon spent building three prompts.

The prompts were the whole trick:

1. Give it a role, not a question
2. Paste in your company headings and standard controls
3. Give it the project facts it cannot possibly know
4. Specify the exact output format
5. Tell it to write [TO BE CONFIRMED] instead of guessing

Two contracts managers got back roughly a day a week each. Rejections from principal contractors dropped, mostly because the wrong-project copy-and-paste errors disappeared.

What did not change: a competent person still reviews and signs every single document. Under CDM 2015 the duty holder owns the content no matter how it was drafted.

The 30-day plan for running this yourself is linked in the comments.

## Post 3 — Short / group version (attach generative-ai-in-construction-hook.png)

Quick one for anyone weighing up AI for their construction business.

The single biggest risk is not that it produces something wrong. It is that it produces something wrong that reads perfectly and gets signed without a proper look.

Generative AI writes with total confidence whether it is right or not. A method statement with a plausible but wrong sequence looks exactly as professional as a correct one.

Get that one control right (a named competent person reviews and owns every output) and the time savings are real and safe.
