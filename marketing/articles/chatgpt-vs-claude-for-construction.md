**For most UK construction teams, Claude is the stronger choice for reading and checking long documents such as tender packs, specifications and contracts, while ChatGPT is the stronger all-rounder for quick drafting, photos, voice notes, images and everyday admin.** Both cost around £18 to £20 a month per person for the paid personal plans, both have free tiers, and both offer business plans with better data controls. The honest answer for many firms is not one or the other: pick one as the team standard for document work and keep the other available for the jobs it does better.

This guide compares the two assistants on the work construction people actually do: tender reading, contract review, RAMS and method statements, site reports, meeting minutes, spreadsheets and photos. It finishes with a worked UK example where both tools were given the same subcontract package, and a short decision guide by role. If you want the detail on either tool on its own, see our pages on [ChatGPT for construction](/ai-tools/chatgpt) and [Claude for construction](/ai-tools/claude).

## The short version

If you only read one section, read this one.

- **Choose Claude** if your day is dominated by long documents: tender packs, specifications, employer's requirements, JCT or NEC contracts, subcontract orders, O&M manuals. It tends to stay closer to the source text, quote clauses accurately and say when something is missing rather than filling the gap.
- **Choose ChatGPT** if your day is dominated by quick, varied tasks: drafting emails, writing a toolbox talk on the van, turning a voice note into a site diary entry, reading a photo of a delivery ticket, or making a quick image for a site notice.
- **Use both** if you can justify around £40 a month for one person, which a commercial manager, estimator or project manager will usually pay back in the first week.
- **Whichever you pick**, use a business or team plan for anything confidential, and keep a competent person checking every output that goes into a contract, a submission or a safety document.

## How they differ in practice

Both tools are large language models with a chat window, file upload, web search and paid tiers. On paper they look almost identical. The differences show up in how they behave on real construction work.

### Reading long documents

This is where Claude has built its reputation. When you upload a 200 page tender pack or a full set of contract amendments, Claude is generally better at keeping the whole document in view and pulling out the specific clause you asked about, with the clause number. It is also more willing to say "the document does not state this" when a detail is genuinely missing.

ChatGPT handles long documents well too, but in our testing it is more inclined to summarise at a higher level and occasionally smooth over gaps with a plausible assumption. For a quick overview that is fine. For a commercial risk review, you want the tool that tells you what is not there.

### Writing and drafting

Both write well. ChatGPT tends to produce punchier, more confident drafts and is quick at turning a few bullet points into a client email or a progress narrative. Claude's drafts are usually more measured and closer to the brief, which suits formal letters, contractual notices and method statement narrative. The difference is stylistic rather than a gap in quality, and good prompting closes most of it. Our [ChatGPT prompts for construction](/guides/chatgpt-prompts-for-construction) work in either tool.

### Photos, voice and images

ChatGPT is ahead on the site side of the job. Its mobile app handles voice conversation well, it reads photos of delivery notes, defects and handwritten notes reliably, and it can generate images, which is useful for site signage mock-ups, newsletter graphics and toolbox talk slides. Claude reads photos and screenshots well but does not generate images.

### Spreadsheets and data

Both can analyse an uploaded Excel or CSV file, write formulas and build tables. ChatGPT has had data analysis tools for longer and is quick at charts. Claude is strong at building structured tables from messy source documents, for example turning three subcontract quotes into one like-for-like comparison. For estimators who live in Excel, also look at [Microsoft Copilot](/ai-tools/microsoft-copilot), which works inside Microsoft 365 directly.

### Team features and data control

Both offer team and enterprise plans where your data is not used to train the models by default, with admin controls, shared workspaces and single sign-on on the higher tiers. ChatGPT has custom GPTs, so you can build a "RAMS checker" or "site diary writer" the whole team uses. Claude has Projects, where you load your standard documents (company RAMS template, house style, standard subcontract amendments) once and every chat in that project uses them. Both approaches work. Check the current terms for whichever plan you buy before uploading client documents.

## Which one by role

### Site managers and supervisors

**ChatGPT first.** The mobile app, voice input and photo reading make it the better tool for the jobs that happen on site: turning a walk-round voice note into a daily report, reading a delivery ticket, drafting a toolbox talk on a specific hazard before the morning briefing. Our [daily site report workflow](/ai-workflows/write-daily-site-report) shows the prompt structure.

### Quantity surveyors, estimators and commercial managers

**Claude first.** Commercial work is reading-heavy and the cost of a missed clause is high. Claude's habit of citing clause numbers and saying when something is absent is exactly what you want when you are checking retention terms, pay-less notice periods under the Housing Grants, Construction and Regeneration Act 1996, or liquidated damages. Use it with our [contract clause summary workflow](/ai-workflows/summarise-contract-clauses) and [tender returns analysis workflow](/ai-workflows/analyse-tender-returns).

### Contracts managers

**Claude first, ChatGPT for correspondence.** Clause comparison and amendment review suit Claude. Quick replies, notices to subcontractors and meeting follow-ups are fast in either. Our [AI for contracts managers](/ai-for/contracts-manager) page has tested prompts for both.

### Health and safety managers

**Either, with a clear template.** Both draft good RAMS, COSHH assessments and toolbox talks when given your company template and the actual scope. Neither replaces the competent person required under CDM 2015 and the Management of Health and Safety at Work Regulations 1999. Load your template, describe the task precisely and check every control measure. Our [method statement workflow](/ai-workflows/draft-method-statement) gives a starting prompt.

### Project managers and directors

**Both, if budget allows.** Project managers swing between document review (programme narratives, change logs, contract correspondence) and fast communication (client updates, minutes, reports). Having both open is the most productive setup we see. For meetings, our [meeting minutes workflow](/ai-workflows/generate-meeting-minutes) works well in either.

## A worked UK example: one subcontract package, both tools

To make the comparison concrete, here is a test based on a typical package review.

**The job:** A 30-person main contractor in Leeds is placing a drylining and partitions package on a £5.2 million school extension under JCT Design and Build 2024. The commercial manager has a 64 page subcontract pack: the main contractor's standard subcontract amendments, the package scope, the specification section for partitions and ceilings, the programme extract and the subcontractor's tender return with a covering letter of qualifications.

**The task:** Both tools were given the same pack and the same prompt: "Review this subcontract package. List every commercial risk to the main contractor, with the document and clause reference. Flag anything the subcontractor has qualified or excluded that conflicts with the scope. Say clearly if information is missing."

### What came back

| Check | ChatGPT | Claude |
|-------|---------|--------|
| Retention (5% in amendments vs 3% in subcontractor's letter) | Found | Found, with both clause references |
| Fire-stopping to head of partitions excluded in qualifications | Found | Found, linked to the spec clause requiring it |
| Pay-less notice period amended to 2 days | Missed | Found |
| No acoustic test requirement stated in the scope | Not mentioned | Flagged as missing information |
| Programme: 14 weeks in tender vs 11 weeks in programme extract | Found | Found |
| Invented risks not in the documents | One (assumed a design responsibility clause that was not there) | None |
| Time to useful output | About 1 minute | About 1 minute |

**The result:** Claude found more of the risks that actually cost money, and crucially did not invent any. ChatGPT's summary was faster to read and well laid out, but the missed pay-less notice amendment and the invented design clause are exactly the errors that matter in a commercial review.

The commercial manager then used ChatGPT to draft the covering email to the subcontractor raising the five points, because it turned the risk list into a clear, polite letter in one go. That split, Claude for reading, ChatGPT for writing it up, is common among the teams we speak to.

One test is not a benchmark, and both tools update frequently. Run the same comparison on one of your own past packages, where you already know the answers, before deciding.

## Where both tools go wrong

The risks are the same whichever you choose.

- **Confident errors.** Both can state something wrong with total confidence. A clause number, a regulation date or a rate can be invented. Check anything that goes into a contract, a submission or a safety document against the source.
- **Out of date regulations.** Building Regulations, the Building Safety Act 2022 regime and HSE guidance change. Ask the tool to work from the document you upload, not its general knowledge, and verify against current gov.uk and HSE sources.
- **Arithmetic in long tables.** Do the sums in Excel. Neither tool should be trusted to add up a long bill of quantities in the chat window.
- **Rates and prices.** Neither knows your labour rates, supplier prices or regional market. Any rate they suggest is a placeholder. Use your own records and benchmark sources such as BCIS from RICS.
- **Confidential documents.** Tender packs and contracts are commercially sensitive. Use business plans, check data settings, and agree what can be uploaded.
- **Competence.** Under CDM 2015, duty holders must be competent. An AI-drafted RAMS or construction phase plan is a draft until a competent person has reviewed and approved it.

## How to decide in a week

1. **Pick three real tasks** you did last month: one long document review, one piece of drafting and one site or admin task.
2. **Run each task in both free tiers** with the same prompt and the same files. Redact anything confidential first.
3. **Score the outputs** on accuracy against the source, time to a usable result, and how much editing they needed.
4. **Pick a team standard** for document work so prompts, templates and Projects or custom GPTs can be shared.
5. **Buy a business plan** for anyone uploading client or tender documents.
6. **Write down the prompts that worked** and share them. That shared library is worth more than the choice of tool.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is ChatGPT or Claude better for construction?

It depends on the job. Claude is generally better for reading and checking long construction documents such as tender packs, specifications and JCT or NEC contracts, because it stays closer to the source text and flags missing information. ChatGPT is generally better for quick drafting, voice notes, reading site photos and generating images. Many UK teams use Claude for document review and ChatGPT for everyday admin.

### Which is better for reviewing JCT and NEC contracts?

Claude tends to perform better on contract review because it cites clause numbers accurately and compares amendments clause by clause. Whichever tool you use, it is a first-pass reviewer, not a replacement for a qualified commercial manager or construction lawyer, and anything that affects your contractual position must be checked against the actual contract documents.

### Can ChatGPT or Claude write RAMS and method statements?

Yes, both can draft good RAMS and method statements if you give them your company template and an accurate description of the task, location, plant and hazards. Neither is a competent person under CDM 2015. Every control measure must be checked by someone with the right knowledge and experience before the document is issued or briefed to operatives.

### How much do ChatGPT and Claude cost in the UK?

Both have free tiers with usage limits. The paid personal plans cost roughly £18 to £20 a month per person. Team and business plans cost more per seat but add admin controls and stronger data protections, which matter if you upload client documents. Prices change regularly, so check each provider's current UK pricing.

### Is it safe to upload tender documents and contracts?

It can be, on the right plan. Business and team plans from both providers generally do not use your data to train their models by default, but check the current terms and your client's confidentiality requirements. Redact names, prices and anything the tender conditions mark as confidential when using free or personal plans.

### Should a small construction firm pay for both?

For one or two people in commercial or management roles, paying for both at around £40 a month each is often worthwhile, because each tool is clearly better at different jobs. For a wider team, pick one as the standard to keep prompts and templates consistent, and give the second tool only to the people who will use it daily.
