**ChatGPT prompts for site managers** are structured instructions that get ChatGPT, Claude or Copilot to handle the paperwork side of running a construction site: turning phone notes into a daily site report, writing a toolbox talk, reviewing a subcontractor's RAMS, building a snag list or drafting a firm email to a trade that is behind. The prompts that work give the AI five things: a site manager role, the real facts from today, the site context (CDM 2015 role, phase, trades), a fixed output format, and an instruction to flag anything missing rather than invent it. The site manager still runs the site and signs off every record. The AI gets the writing done in minutes instead of at 7pm in the cabin.

This guide gives you 22 copy-paste prompts grouped by the rhythm of a site week, a worked UK example of a daily report built from rough notes, and the rules that keep AI-drafted site records accurate enough to rely on in a dispute. For the wider picture of AI in the role, start with our [site manager hub](/ai-for/site-manager).

## Why site managers get the most from AI (and the most risk)

A site manager produces more written records than almost anyone on a project, most of it written at the end of a long day, from memory. Most of that work is turning facts you already have into a standard format, which is exactly what AI does well. But site records have three features that make lazy prompts dangerous:

- **Site records are evidence.** A daily report is the contemporaneous record that supports or defeats a delay claim, a variation or an accident investigation. If the AI adds a detail that did not happen, you have created a false record with your name on it.
- **Safety documents carry legal duties.** Under CDM 2015 the principal contractor must plan, manage and monitor the construction phase. A toolbox talk or briefing that sounds right but misses the actual hazard on your site is worse than useless.
- **Subcontractor communication has commercial weight.** An email that reads like an instruction can become a variation. Tone and wording matter.

So every prompt below supplies the facts, names the context, fixes the format and includes a guardrail.

## The site manager prompt formula

| Part | What it does | Example wording |
|------|--------------|-----------------|
| **Role** | Sets site expertise and UK context | "You are an experienced site manager working for a UK main contractor." |
| **Facts** | Gives the real data from today | "Today: 14 operatives on site, ground floor slab poured zone B, 2 hours lost to rain." |
| **Context** | Names the phase, contract and CDM role | "We are principal contractor under CDM 2015. Frame stage, JCT Design and Build 2024." |
| **Format** | Fixes the output shape | "Use these headings: weather, labour, plant, deliveries, progress, delays, instructions, H&S." |
| **Guardrails** | Stops invention | "Use only the facts I give. Write [CONFIRM] where anything is missing. Do not add events, names or quantities." |

The guardrail is the line that matters most. On site records, "use only what I give you" is not a nice-to-have. It is what keeps the record honest.

### Getting your notes into the AI

Site managers rarely sit at a desk, so the input matters as much as the prompt. Three methods work well:

- **Voice memo, then transcript.** Talk through the day on your phone as you walk the site, then paste the transcript. Most phones and the ChatGPT and Claude apps transcribe speech directly.
- **Bullet notes in your phone's notes app.** Short lines through the day ("10:20 Hilti delivery 40 mins late", "Smith Groundworks 4 men not 6"). These give the AI the times and numbers it needs.
- **Photos with captions.** ChatGPT, Claude and Copilot can describe what is in a photo, but always tell the AI what it is looking at and where. Never let it guess the location or the trade.

## Daily records prompts

### 1. Daily site report from rough notes

> You are a UK construction site manager. Turn my rough notes below into a daily site report with these headings: date, weather (morning and afternoon, any time lost), labour on site by trade and company, plant on site, deliveries (supplier, item, time, any issues), work completed by location, delays and their causes, instructions received (who gave them, verbal or written), health and safety (incidents, near misses, toolbox talks, visitors), and items for tomorrow. Use only my notes. Write [CONFIRM] where information is missing. Do not invent quantities, names or times. Notes: [paste]

## Health and safety prompts

Under CDM 2015, the principal contractor must provide suitable site inductions, information and instruction. AI is excellent at turning a hazard into a clear briefing, but you must give it the real hazard and check the controls match your RAMS.

### 5. Toolbox talk on a specific hazard

> Write a 10-minute toolbox talk for operatives on a UK construction site about [hazard, e.g. working near an open excavation]. Structure: why it matters (one real-world consequence), the specific hazards on our site, the controls we use, what to do if something goes wrong, and 3 questions to check understanding. Plain English, short sentences, no jargon. Our site specifics: [paste]

### 6. Site induction briefing

> Draft the spoken script for a 15-minute site induction for new starters on this project. Cover: site rules, the principal contractor and site management team, welfare locations, emergency procedures and assembly point, first aiders, permits in use, PPE requirements, current high-risk activities, and how to report a near miss. Mark anything I have not given you as [CONFIRM]. Site details: [paste]

### 7. RAMS review checklist

> You are a UK site manager reviewing a subcontractor's RAMS before work starts. Review the document below and list: hazards that are missing for the task described, controls that are generic rather than specific to our site, missing sequence steps, missing emergency or rescue arrangements, competence or training gaps, and anything that conflicts with our site rules. Do not rewrite the RAMS. Return a numbered list of comments I can send back. RAMS: [paste]

## Subcontractor management prompts

### 14. Firm but fair email to a trade that is behind

> Write an email to a subcontractor who is behind programme. Facts: [paste]. State what was planned, what has been achieved, the impact on following trades, and what we need from them (resource levels and a recovery date) by a set deadline. Firm, professional, UK business tone. Do not threaten, do not mention money, and do not give any instruction that changes their scope.

## Prompt quality compared: weak vs strong

| | Weak prompt | Strong prompt |
|---|-------------|---------------|
| **Example** | "Write today's site report." | Prompt 1 above, with your real notes pasted in |
| **Facts** | None, so the AI invents labour and progress | Real times, numbers and events from your notes |
| **Context** | No phase, no CDM role, no trades | Phase, contract, principal contractor role named |
| **Format** | Whatever the AI decides | Your company's report headings |
| **Guardrails** | None | "Use only my notes", "[CONFIRM] where missing" |
| **Evidence value** | Low: contains invented detail | High: factual, time-stamped, same day |
| **Time to finished record** | 20 to 30 minutes of correcting | 5 minutes of checking |

## Worked example: a daily site report from phone notes

Here is prompt 1 on a real-style job.

**The project:** 42 new-build apartments over four storeys in Leeds, timber frame on a concrete podium. JCT Design and Build 2024. The main contractor is principal contractor under CDM 2015. Week 23 of a 64-week programme.

**The site manager's phone notes, typed through the day:**

- Dry am, heavy rain 13:00 to 15:00, frame crew stood down 2 hrs
- Frame crew 6, groundworks 4 (supposed to be 6), M&E 3, scaffold 2
- Telehandler, 1 x 25t mobile crane am only
- Timber frame panels block B level 2 arrived 08:10, 1 panel damaged, rejected, photo 114
- Block B L2 frame 60% done, block A drainage run 3 complete
- Client's rep said on walk round move kitchen window to suit new layout plot 14
- Near miss: unsecured scaffold board L3, removed, toolbox talk 16:00
- Tomorrow: crane 07:30, roof trusses block A

**What the AI produced (extract):**

**Weather:** Dry in the morning. Heavy rain from 13:00 to 15:00. Timber frame crew stood down for 2 hours.

**Labour:** Timber frame: 6. Groundworks: 4 (programmed: 6, shortfall of 2). M&E: 3. Scaffolding: 2. Total: 15. [CONFIRM company names]

**Deliveries:** Timber frame panels for block B level 2 received at 08:10. One panel damaged on arrival and rejected (photo 114). [CONFIRM supplier name and replacement date]

**Instructions received:** Verbal instruction from the client's representative during the site walk round: move the kitchen window on plot 14 to suit a new layout. Written confirmation to be requested. [CONFIRM name of representative and time]

**Health and safety:** One near miss: unsecured scaffold board at level 3. Board removed immediately. Toolbox talk delivered at 16:00. No RIDDOR reportable incidents. [CONFIRM attendance numbers]

Look at the verbal instruction. The AI recorded the facts and asked for the name, but did not decide whether it was a variation. That is the right behaviour. The site manager then used prompt 3 to send a confirmation of verbal instruction email the same evening, the record the QS will need if this becomes a cost.

Total time: about 5 minutes from notes to a checked report, against the 25 to 40 minutes most site managers spend by hand. Across a week that is over two hours back.

## Rules for using AI safely as a site manager

- **Never let AI add facts.** Every line in a site record must be something you saw, heard or were told. If the AI adds anything you did not give it, delete it.
- **Write it the same day.** A daily report drafted with AI at 5pm is a contemporaneous record. One drafted from memory three days later is not, however well written.
- **Keep your raw notes and photos.** The original notes are the evidence. The AI version sits on top of them, not in place of them.
- **Do not outsource safety judgement.** AI can write a clear toolbox talk or flag gaps in a RAMS, but a competent person must decide whether controls are adequate. Under CDM 2015 that duty stays with you and your company.
- **Use approved tools only.** Check your company's AI policy. Remove personal data such as operatives' names and phone numbers before pasting if you are unsure.
- **Save the prompts that work.** Build a short library in your notes app for your company's report formats. Small edits each day beat starting from a blank chat.

## Frequently asked questions

### What are the best ChatGPT prompts for construction site managers?

The most useful prompts cover the paperwork that eats a site manager's evening: the daily site report, site diary entries, toolbox talks, RAMS reviews, snag lists, look-ahead summaries and emails to subcontractors. The best prompts give the AI a site manager role, the real facts from today, the site context such as your CDM 2015 role, a fixed output format and an instruction to write [CONFIRM] rather than guess.

### Can ChatGPT write a daily site report?

Yes. Give it your rough notes or a voice memo transcript and a set of headings, and it will produce a structured daily site report in under a minute. You must check every line against what actually happened, fill in anything marked [CONFIRM] and keep your original notes. Done the same day, it is a strong contemporaneous record.

### Are AI-written site records valid evidence in a dispute?

The value of a site record comes from being accurate, detailed and written at the time, not from who typed it. An AI-formatted report built only from your same-day notes, checked and signed by you, is as good as a handwritten one and usually more complete. A report where the AI has added details that did not happen is a liability, which is why the guardrail line matters.

### Can I use ChatGPT to write toolbox talks and RAMS?

You can use it to draft toolbox talks and to review RAMS for gaps, and it is very good at both. It cannot see your site, so you must give it the real hazards and check the controls against your actual method and risk assessment. A competent person must approve any safety document before it is used, as CDM 2015 requires.

### Which is better for site managers: ChatGPT, Claude or Copilot?

All three work with the prompts in this guide. ChatGPT and Claude both have strong mobile apps with voice input, which suits site work. Claude handles long documents such as full RAMS or programme exports particularly well. If your company runs Microsoft 365, Copilot may already be approved. The prompt structure matters more than the tool. See our [Claude guide](/ai-tools/claude) for more.

### How much time can AI save a site manager?

Most site managers save the most on daily reports, toolbox talks, minutes and subcontractor emails. A daily report that took 30 minutes typically takes 5 including checking, and a toolbox talk drops from an hour to 10 minutes. Across a week that is often three to five hours back for being on site, which is where the job actually gets done.
