The **golden thread** is the accurate, up-to-date, digital record of information about a building that shows it was designed and built safely and lets the people responsible keep it safe once occupied. It became a legal requirement in England for higher-risk buildings under the Building Safety Act 2022, with the detailed duties in force from 1 October 2023. In plain terms: if you design, build or manage a residential building 18 metres or 7 storeys and above, you must keep a controlled, digital, single source of truth for its safety information, and hand it over intact.

The phrase comes from Dame Judith Hackitt's 2018 review, Building a Safer Future, which followed the Grenfell Tower fire. Her finding was that nobody could reliably say what had been built, what had been changed, or why. The golden thread is the fix: a record strong enough that anyone can pick it up years later and trust it.

This guide explains what the golden thread contains, who owns it at each stage, how it ties into the three gateways, the mistakes that cause Gateway 2 and Gateway 3 delays, and how AI can take the grind out of keeping it accurate.

## What the golden thread actually is (and is not)

The golden thread is not a single document, and it is not a piece of software. It is both a body of information and the way that information is managed. The Building Safety Regulator (BSR) guidance describes it as information that is kept digitally, securely, accurately and in a way that the right people can find and understand it when they need it.

It is easier to understand by what it must do:

- **Show compliance.** Demonstrate that the building as designed and as built meets the Building Regulations, especially on fire and structural safety.
- **Record change.** Capture every change made during construction, why it was made, and who approved it.
- **Support safe occupation.** Give the Accountable Person what they need to manage building safety risks for the life of the building.
- **Survive handover.** Pass from the construction team to the people who run the building without information being lost.

What it is **not**: a dump of every email and photo on the project. The test is relevance: does this information help someone understand or manage the safety of the building?

## Which buildings need a golden thread?

The statutory golden thread applies to **higher-risk buildings (HRBs)** in England. For the design and construction phase, an HRB is a building that:

- is at least **18 metres** in height **or** has at least **7 storeys**, and
- contains at least **2 residential units**, or is a hospital or care home (during design and construction).

Once occupied, the building safety regime (and the golden thread duties that sit with the Accountable Person) applies to occupied HRBs with at least two residential units.

Below that threshold there is no statutory golden thread, but the principles are still good practice, and many clients and funders now ask for golden-thread-style information on lower-rise residential and mixed-use schemes anyway. If you are already working to the [CDM 2015 construction phase plan](/guides/cdm-construction-phase-plan-guide) discipline, you are part of the way there.

## Who is responsible for the golden thread?

The Building Safety Act deliberately mirrors CDM 2015. The same three roles that hold design and construction duties under CDM also hold Building Regulations dutyholder duties, and with them responsibility for the golden thread.

| Role | Main golden thread responsibility | When |
|------|-----------------------------------|------|
| **Client** | Ensures the golden thread is set up, kept and handed over; appoints competent dutyholders; submits Gateway 2 and Gateway 3 applications | Throughout design and construction |
| **Principal designer** | Plans, manages and monitors design work for Building Regulations compliance; keeps design information in the thread | Design and construction |
| **Principal contractor** | Plans, manages and monitors building work for compliance; records as-built information and change control | Construction |
| **Designers and contractors** | Provide accurate information on their own work and changes | Their own packages |
| **Accountable Person / Principal Accountable Person** | Keeps the golden thread up to date for the life of the building; uses it to manage building safety risks | Occupation |
| **Building Safety Regulator** | Building control authority for HRBs; assesses Gateway 2 and Gateway 3 applications | Gateways and inspections |

Note that the Building Regulations principal designer and principal contractor are distinct roles from the CDM principal designer and principal contractor, though on most projects the same organisations hold both. Make sure appointments state clearly which roles each organisation holds.

## The golden thread and the three gateways

The golden thread is built in layers as the project moves through the three gateways.

### Gateway 1: planning

Gateway 1 applies at planning stage. The applicant submits a fire statement with the planning application, setting out how fire safety has been considered in the site layout, access for the fire service and water supplies. This is where the thread starts, even though the formal golden thread duties bite hardest from Gateway 2.

### Gateway 2: before construction starts

Work on an HRB cannot start until the BSR has approved the building control application. This is the single biggest pinch point in the regime. The application must show, in detail, how the building will comply. It typically includes:

- full plans and specifications
- a competence declaration for the dutyholders
- a construction control plan, explaining how compliance will be maintained during the build
- a change control plan, explaining how changes will be recorded and managed
- a mandatory occurrence reporting plan
- a fire and emergency file strategy and a description of how the golden thread will be kept

The statutory decision period for a new HRB is 12 weeks, though in practice many projects have waited considerably longer, often because applications were incomplete or inconsistent. A well-organised golden thread from day one is the best defence against delay.

### During construction: change control

Once building work starts, every change has to be handled through the change control process agreed at Gateway 2. Changes are categorised:

- **Major changes** need a formal application and BSR approval before the changed work proceeds.
- **Notifiable changes** must be notified to the BSR, with supporting information, before or shortly after they proceed (depending on the change).
- **Other changes** are recorded in the change control log.

The change control log is the heart of the golden thread during construction. It is also where most projects slip. Substituted products, revised details and late design development pile up, and if they are not logged with the reason and approval, the thread breaks.

### Gateway 3: completion

Before the building can be occupied, the BSR must issue a completion certificate. The Gateway 3 application must include the as-built information, the final change control log, confirmation of compliance from the principal designer and principal contractor, and the golden thread information that will be handed to the Accountable Person. If the thread is patchy, Gateway 3 stalls and the building cannot be occupied.

## What goes into the golden thread

The precise content depends on the building, but the core is consistent. Use this as a working checklist.

| Information | Typical source | Stage added |
|-------------|----------------|-------------|
| Fire statement | Fire engineer / client | Gateway 1 |
| Approved plans and specifications | Principal designer | Gateway 2 |
| Fire and emergency file | Fire engineer, principal designer | Gateway 2, updated to Gateway 3 |
| Construction control plan | Principal contractor | Gateway 2 |
| Change control plan and change control log | Principal contractor, principal designer | Gateway 2 onwards |
| Mandatory occurrence reports | Principal contractor | Construction |
| Product and material information (including fire performance data) | Contractors, suppliers | Construction |
| Compliance evidence, inspection and test records | Contractors, inspectors | Construction |
| Competence declarations and dutyholder appointments | Client | Gateway 2, updated |
| As-built drawings and specifications | Principal contractor | Gateway 3 |
| Compartmentation and fire stopping records (with photos) | Specialist contractors | Construction |
| Key building information submitted at registration | Accountable Person | Occupation |

Two items cause more trouble than any others: **product substitutions** (where a specified product is swapped for an "equivalent" without the fire performance evidence being captured) and **fire stopping records** (where penetrations are sealed but not photographed or recorded against a location). Both are invisible once the walls are closed up, which is why they must be captured at the time.

## The golden thread principles

Government guidance sets out a set of principles that the golden thread should meet. Put simply, the information should be:

- **Accurate and trusted.** People can rely on it without re-checking everything.
- **Kept digitally.** Not paper files in a site cabin.
- **A single source of truth.** One version, not five conflicting copies.
- **Secure.** Protected from loss, unauthorised changes and cyber risk.
- **Accountable.** You can see who added or changed what, and when.
- **Understandable and accessible.** The right people can find and read it.
- **Relevant and proportionate.** Useful information, not everything.
- **Durable and interoperable.** It survives handover, software changes and changes of owner, ideally using open, non-proprietary formats.

No particular software or BIM maturity level is mandated. A well-structured common data environment or a carefully controlled document management system can both satisfy the principles. Information scattered across inboxes and WhatsApp groups cannot. ## A worked golden thread example

Here is a realistic example of how a single change flows through the golden thread on a UK project.

**Project:** Kirkgate Yard, Leeds. Nine-storey residential block, 64 flats, top floor at 27 metres. Higher-risk building. Design and build contract. Gateway 2 approved in March 2026.

**The change:** In week 22, the cladding subcontractor reports a 14-week lead time on the specified mineral wool cavity barrier. They propose an alternative product from a different manufacturer, which they describe as "equivalent".

**What a broken thread looks like:** The site team accepts the alternative by email to keep the programme moving. The substitution is not logged. At Gateway 3, the as-built specification still names the original product, the photos show a different product, and nobody can produce the test evidence for the alternative in the tested configuration. Gateway 3 is delayed while the team reconstructs the evidence, and at worst opening up is required.

**What a working thread looks like:**

1. The principal contractor raises a change request, reference CR-047, the same day.
2. The principal designer reviews it. Because it affects fire performance of the external wall, the change is assessed against the change control plan and categorised as notifiable (the team takes advice and records the reasoning).
3. The fire engineer reviews the alternative product's test evidence and confirms it is suitable in the tested configuration, with the classification report attached to CR-047.
4. The BSR is notified in line with the change control plan.
5. The change control log, specification and drawing register are updated, with revision references.
6. Installation is photographed by location (elevation, floor, grid line) and the photos are linked to CR-047.
7. At Gateway 3, the as-built information, change log and evidence all agree.

Total extra effort at the time: perhaps two to three hours across the team. Effort saved at Gateway 3: potentially weeks.

### Where AI helped on this project

The document controller used an AI assistant (in this case [Claude](/ai-tools/claude)) for three tasks around CR-047:

- **Drafting the change record.** The site manager's rough notes and the subcontractor's email were turned into a structured change request with the description, reason, affected drawings, and the questions the principal designer needed to answer.
- **Cross-checking registers.** The change log, drawing register and specification index were exported to CSV and the AI was asked to list every document that still referenced the original product. It found four, including a fire strategy appendix the team had missed.
- **Summarising the evidence.** The 40-page classification report was summarised into the key test parameters, so the fire engineer could confirm quickly which configuration had been tested.

The AI did not decide whether the change was notifiable, did not approve the product, and did not sign anything. It handled the paperwork and the cross-checking. People made the decisions.

## How AI can help keep the golden thread accurate

Most golden thread failures are caused by volume, not bad intent. A busy HRB project generates thousands of documents and hundreds of changes, and that is exactly where AI earns its place.

### Tasks AI does well

- **Turning rough notes into structured records.** Change requests, mandatory occurrence report drafts, inspection records and handover summaries, all in a consistent format.
- **Register cross-checks.** Comparing the change log, drawing register and specification to find mismatches and documents that were never updated.
- **Summarising long documents.** Fire strategies, test reports and specifications condensed into the facts a reviewer needs.
- **Gap checks before a gateway.** Comparing what you have against a Gateway 2 or Gateway 3 checklist and listing what is missing.
- **Plain-English handover summaries.** Turning technical information into something the Accountable Person and residents can understand.

You can find ready-made prompts for this sort of work in our [AI workflows library](/ai-workflows), and the [contract clause summary workflow](/ai-workflows/summarise-contract-clauses) is useful for checking which party carries which Building Safety Act duties in your appointments.

### Tasks AI must not do

- **Making compliance decisions.** Whether a change is major or notifiable, whether a product is suitable, whether the building complies: those are judgements for competent people.
- **Filling gaps with guesses.** If test evidence is missing, the AI must say so, never invent it. Give it one standing instruction: flag gaps, never fill them.
- **Replacing the golden thread system.** AI is a tool for working on the information, not a place to keep it. The record lives in your controlled system.

Golden thread information can include security-relevant detail about a residential building, so use business AI accounts with data controls and check your client's information security requirements first.

## Common golden thread mistakes

- **Starting at Gateway 3.** Assembling the thread at the end of the job is the most expensive way to do it. Set it up before Gateway 2.
- **Unlogged product substitutions.** Every substitution goes through change control, however small it looks.
- **Multiple versions of the truth.** One source, controlled revisions, no marked-up copies in the site cabin.
- **Unclear dutyholder appointments.** If nobody knows who is the Building Regulations principal designer, nobody owns the design side of the thread.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the golden thread in construction?

The golden thread is the digital, accurate and up-to-date record of information that shows a higher-risk building was designed and built to comply with the Building Regulations, and that allows it to be managed safely once occupied. It was recommended by the Hackitt review in 2018 and made a legal requirement for higher-risk buildings in England by the Building Safety Act 2022, with the detailed duties in force from October 2023.

### Which buildings need a golden thread?

In England, the statutory golden thread applies to higher-risk buildings: those at least 18 metres high or with at least 7 storeys, containing at least 2 residential units (and, during design and construction, hospitals and care homes meeting the height test). Lower-rise buildings do not have a statutory golden thread, but many clients now ask for the same discipline as good practice.

### Who is responsible for the golden thread?

During design and construction, the client is responsible for ensuring the golden thread is created, maintained and handed over, with the Building Regulations principal designer and principal contractor managing the design and construction information. After completion, the Accountable Person (and the Principal Accountable Person where there is more than one) keeps it up to date for the life of the building.

### Does the golden thread have to use BIM?

No. The golden thread must be digital, secure, accessible and kept as a single source of truth, but no particular software or BIM level is mandated. A well-run common data environment is a natural fit, but a disciplined document management system with clear naming, revision control and a change log can also meet the requirements. Open, non-proprietary formats are strongly recommended so the information survives handover.

### What happens if the golden thread is incomplete?

At Gateway 3, the Building Safety Regulator can refuse or delay the completion certificate if the information does not demonstrate compliance, which means the building cannot be occupied. In serious cases opening up of finished work may be needed to prove what was installed. The Building Safety Act also created criminal offences for dutyholders who breach their duties, so an incomplete thread is a commercial and legal risk.

### Can AI help manage the golden thread?

Yes, for the information work. AI is good at turning rough notes into structured change records, cross-checking change logs against drawing and specification registers, summarising long fire strategies and test reports, and running gap checks before a gateway. It must not make compliance decisions or fill missing information with guesses, and the record itself must live in your controlled system.
