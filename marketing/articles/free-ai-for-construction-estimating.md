**Free AI for construction estimating** means using the no-cost versions of general AI assistants (ChatGPT, Claude, Google Gemini and Microsoft Copilot) to speed up the reading, writing, structuring and checking work that surrounds an estimate. The free tiers are genuinely useful: they can summarise a tender pack, draft a bill of quantities layout, normalise subcontract quotes and spot gaps in your scope. What they cannot do is price the job for you, because they do not know your rates, your supply chain or current UK market costs.

This guide is for estimators, quantity surveyors and small contractors who want to try AI before paying for anything. It covers which free tools are worth using, the limits you will hit, a worked UK example on a small extension, and the exact prompts to copy. If you want the full picture of AI across the estimating process, including paid options, read our main guide to [AI construction estimating](/guides/ai-construction-estimating) first.

## Can you really estimate with free AI?

Yes, as long as you are clear about which part of the job you are handing over. An estimate has three layers:

1. **Understanding the scope.** Reading drawings, specifications, preliminaries and tender instructions to work out what is actually being asked for.
2. **Measuring and structuring.** Turning that scope into quantities, organised into a sensible breakdown (elements, work sections or NRM2 headings).
3. **Pricing and judgement.** Applying labour, plant, materials and subcontract rates, then adding preliminaries, overheads, profit and risk.

Free AI is strong on layer one, helpful on layer two and weak on layer three. The good news is that layers one and two are where estimators lose most of their hours. Reading a 200-page tender pack, chasing missing information and building the skeleton of a bill are slow, low-judgement tasks. That is exactly where a free assistant earns its keep.

The pricing layer stays with you. Your rates come from your own cost records, your suppliers and your subcontractors, and no free chatbot has access to them. Treat any rate an AI suggests as a placeholder to be replaced, never as a number to submit.

## The free AI tools worth using for estimating

There are four general assistants with free tiers that are worth an estimator's time. All four can read text you paste in, and most can read uploaded PDFs, spreadsheets and images within daily limits.

### ChatGPT (free)

[ChatGPT](/ai-tools/chatgpt) is the most familiar option. The free tier handles long pasted text well, can read uploaded files within a daily allowance, and is good at turning messy notes into tidy tables. It is a solid all-rounder for drafting queries to the client, structuring a bill and writing tender narrative.

### Claude (free)

[Claude](/ai-tools/claude) is particularly good at reading long documents carefully and following detailed instructions. For estimators, that makes it a strong choice for tender pack reviews, specification summaries and scope gap checks, where you want the AI to stay faithful to the source text rather than improvise. The free tier has a usage cap that resets every few hours, so batch your questions.

### Google Gemini (free)

[Google Gemini](/ai-tools/google-gemini) is useful if your team already works in Google Sheets and Drive. It is reasonable at summarising documents and drafting text, and it handles images of drawings and site photos.

### Microsoft Copilot (free)

The free version of [Microsoft Copilot](/ai-tools/microsoft-copilot) is a reasonable general assistant. Note that the Copilot built into Excel, which can work directly on your spreadsheets, is part of paid Microsoft 365 plans rather than the free tier.

## What free AI does well in estimating

### Reading and summarising tender packs

Paste in or upload the tender instructions, preliminaries and specification, and ask for a structured summary: form of contract, programme, key dates, insurances, retention, liquidated damages, design responsibility and any unusual clauses. This takes a free AI a minute and can save an estimator half a day on a larger pack.

### Finding scope gaps and missing information

Ask the AI to list everything the documents leave unclear: missing drawings, undefined finishes, provisional sums with no description, items shown on drawings but absent from the specification. The output becomes your list of tender queries.

### Building the bill of quantities skeleton

Free AI is good at producing the structure of a bill. Give it the scope and ask for item descriptions organised by element or NRM2 work section, with units of measurement but blank quantities and rates. You then fill in the measured quantities from your take-off. Our [bill of quantities workflow](/ai-workflows/generate-bill-of-quantities) has a tested prompt for this.

### Comparing subcontract quotes

Subcontractor quotes rarely arrive in the same format. Paste three groundworks quotes into a free AI and ask it to put them in a side-by-side table showing inclusions, exclusions, assumptions, programme and validity. It will catch the exclusion buried on page three that changes the comparison. For a fuller process, use the [tender returns analysis workflow](/ai-workflows/analyse-tender-returns).

### Early cost plans and order-of-cost estimates

For feasibility work, AI can help structure an elemental cost plan in line with NRM1 and draft the assumptions and exclusions. You still supply the cost per square metre from your own data or published benchmarks such as BCIS. Our [cost plan template](/templates/cost-plan) and the [preliminary cost plan workflow](/ai-workflows/generate-preliminary-cost-plan) give you a starting format.

### Writing the tender submission

Method statements for the tender, quality responses, programme narratives and covering letters are all text, and free AI drafts them well when you feed it the project specifics.

## What free AI gets wrong

Knowing the failure modes is what separates useful AI estimating from dangerous AI estimating.

- **It invents rates.** Ask a free chatbot what blockwork costs per square metre in Manchester and it will give you a confident number with no reliable source. Never use it.
- **It does arithmetic badly on long lists.** Language models can slip when adding up 80 line items. Do all extensions and totals in Excel or Google Sheets, then ask the AI to check for obvious anomalies.
- **It cannot measure reliably from drawings.** Free AI can describe a drawing and read the dimensions printed on it, but it does not do accurate scaled take-off. Use proper take-off software or a scale rule.
- **It misses things it was not shown.** If you only paste the specification, it will not know what is on the drawings. The output is only as complete as the input.
- **It may not follow NRM2 exactly.** It knows the general structure, but check measurement rules and units against the standard yourself.

## Is it safe to put tender documents into free AI?

This is the question estimators ask most, and it deserves a straight answer. On free consumer tiers, your conversations may be used to train future models unless you switch that setting off. ChatGPT, Claude and Gemini all provide a setting to opt out of training on consumer accounts; find it and turn it off before uploading anything.

Even with training switched off, apply some simple rules:

- **Check the tender conditions.** Many invitations to tender include confidentiality clauses. Uploading client documents to a third-party service may breach them.
- **Strip names before pasting.** Replace the client name, site address and project name with neutral labels. The AI does not need them to summarise a specification.
- **Never upload personal data** such as named operatives, CSCS card numbers or contact details.
- **Consider a business plan for live tenders.** Paid team and business plans typically exclude your data from training by default and give you admin controls. For regular commercial use on confidential work, that is usually worth the money.

## A UK worked example: pricing a single-storey rear extension

Here is how a small building contractor in Leeds used only free AI tools and a spreadsheet to put together a tender for a 24 m² single-storey rear extension with a flat roof, priced under a JCT Minor Works contract.

**Step 1: Tender pack summary (Claude, free).** The estimator removed the client's name and address, then uploaded the architect's specification (14 pages) and the tender letter. Prompt: "Summarise this tender pack for a building contractor. List the contract form, key dates, scope of works by element, items the contractor must design, provisional sums, and anything unusual." The summary flagged a contractor-designed warm roof and a provisional sum of £1,500 for drainage diversion that had no description.

**Step 2: Tender queries (ChatGPT, free).** The estimator pasted the summary and the drawing notes and asked: "List every item that is unclear or missing that a contractor would need to clarify before pricing." The AI returned nine queries, including the specified rooflight size (shown on plan but missing from the specification), the extent of the drainage diversion and whether party wall matters were resolved. Six were sent to the architect.

**Step 3: Bill skeleton (ChatGPT, free).** Prompt: "Create a bill of quantities structure for this extension, grouped by element (substructure, frame and upper floors, roof, external walls, windows and doors, internal finishes, services, drainage, external works). Give item descriptions and units only. Leave quantities and rates blank." The estimator copied the table into Excel and filled in quantities from their own take-off: 9.6 m³ of concrete trench fill, 48 m² of cavity wall, 24 m² of warm deck roof and so on.

**Step 4: Pricing (Excel, no AI).** Rates came from the contractor's own recent jobs and three supplier quotes. The direct cost came to £41,200. Preliminaries (site set-up, skip hire, welfare, scaffolding and supervision over an eight-week programme) added £5,800, and overheads and profit at 12% brought the tender to £52,640, plus the £1,500 provisional sum.

**Step 5: Subcontract comparison (Claude, free).** Three roofing quotes were pasted in with company names removed. The AI table showed the cheapest quote excluded the upstand detailing to the rooflight and the 20-year guarantee, which made the middle quote the better value once adjusted.

**Step 6: Sense check (ChatGPT, free).** The estimator pasted the priced summary (not the full bill) and asked: "Check this extension estimate for missing items or anything that looks unusually low or high for a UK domestic extension." It flagged that building control fees and the party wall surveyor were not shown. Building control was already in the client's budget, but the estimator added a clarification line on party wall fees.

**Result:** the estimator reckoned the AI steps saved about three hours on a job that normally takes a day to price, and the tender went out with a cleaner set of clarifications. Total software cost: nothing beyond the Excel licence the business already had.

## Copy-ready prompts for free AI estimating

These prompts work on the free tier of any of the main assistants. Replace the bracketed text with your own information.

**Tender summary:**
"You are an experienced UK estimator. Summarise the attached tender documents. Give me: contract form and amendments, key dates, scope by element, contractor's design portion, provisional sums, insurances, retention, liquidated damages, and any onerous clauses. Use headings and bullet points."

**Scope gap check:**
"Compare the drawings notes and the specification below. List any item that appears in one but not the other, any finish or product that is not fully specified, and any quantity that cannot be measured from the information given. Format as a numbered list of tender queries."

**Bill structure:**
"Create a bill of quantities structure for [project description], following NRM2 work sections. For each item give a description and unit of measurement. Leave quantity and rate columns blank. Output as a table I can paste into Excel."

**Quote comparison:**
"Put these [number] subcontract quotes for [package] into a comparison table showing price, inclusions, exclusions, assumptions, programme, payment terms and validity. Then list what I need to add to each quote to compare them on a like-for-like basis."

**Estimate sense check:**
"Review this estimate summary for a [project type] in [region]. List anything that appears to be missing, and any element that looks out of proportion to the others. Do not suggest rates. Explain your reasoning."

## When free stops being enough

Free tiers are a good way to learn, but most regular users hit a ceiling within a few weeks. The signs you should upgrade:

- **You keep running out of messages** mid-way through a tender review.
- **You need to upload large packs**, such as a full set of drawings and a 300-page specification, in one go.
- **You are working on confidential tenders** and need business-grade data controls.
- **Several estimators** need shared prompts, projects and consistent outputs.

At that point, one paid assistant at around £18 to £20 a month per estimator is usually the best value upgrade. Dedicated estimating software with AI features (take-off tools and estimating platforms) is a bigger investment and makes sense when the AI is working on measurement data you already hold in that system. ## Where free AI fits in the UK estimating framework

Free AI does not replace the established UK framework; it sits inside it.

- **RICS NRM1** governs order-of-cost estimates and cost plans. AI can structure the elemental breakdown and draft assumptions, but the cost data must be yours or from a recognised source.
- **RICS NRM2** governs detailed measurement for works procurement. AI can lay out the work sections and item descriptions; you remain responsible for the measurement rules.
- **CDM 2015** requires the principal contractor to plan, manage and monitor the construction phase. Your tender should price the preliminaries that make that possible, such as welfare, supervision and temporary works. AI is useful for checking you have not left these out, which is one of the most common omissions on small jobs.
- **Contract terms** (JCT or NEC) affect risk pricing. AI can summarise the clauses that matter, but the commercial judgement on how much risk to carry is yours.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the best free AI for construction estimating?

For reading and checking tender documents, the free version of Claude is very strong because it follows long documents carefully. For drafting bills, queries and tender text, the free version of ChatGPT is an excellent all-rounder. Many estimators use both, alongside Excel for the actual pricing.

### Can free AI produce a complete construction estimate?

No. Free AI can summarise the tender, structure the bill, compare quotes and check for missing items, but it does not know your rates or current market prices. Any rate it suggests should be treated as unreliable. The pricing and final judgement must come from the estimator.

### Is free AI accurate enough for estimating?

It is accurate enough for reading, structuring and checking, provided you verify the output. It is not accurate enough for arithmetic on long bills or for measurement from drawings. Do totals in a spreadsheet and take-off with proper tools.

### Will free AI follow NRM2?

It understands the general structure of NRM2 and can organise a bill into its work sections, but it can get measurement rules and units wrong. Always check the output against the standard before issuing a bill.

### Is it safe to upload tender documents to free AI tools?

Only with care. Switch off model training in the settings, check the tender for confidentiality clauses, and remove client names, addresses and personal data before uploading. For regular work on confidential tenders, a paid business plan with stronger data controls is the safer choice.

### How much time can free AI save an estimator?

On small and medium jobs, estimators typically report saving two to four hours per tender, mostly on document review, queries and bill structure.
