## When not to use it
Don't use it to classify hazardous waste. Asbestos, contaminated soils and mixed hazardous materials need a competent person and the right surveys, such as a refurbishment and demolition asbestos survey before strip-out. The plan can record the arrangements. It can't make those decisions.

Be careful with the quantities too. The prompt asks for UK benchmarks where you don't have estimates. Treat any figure the tool produces as a placeholder until you've checked it against your own records or your waste contractor's data.

## Common mistakes
- Using waste carriers or receiving sites without checking their registrations and permits. If your waste ends up fly-tipped, you can still be held responsible.
- Planning more segregated skips than the compound can take.
- Mixing plasterboard in with general waste. Gypsum needs keeping separate.
- Wrong or generic waste codes on transfer notes, especially for soils and other materials that can be hazardous or not depending on what's in them.
- Writing the plan and never updating it. Record actual waste through the job and compare it with the forecast.

## FAQs
### Is a site waste management plan a legal requirement in England?
Not as a general rule any more. The regulations that made them compulsory in England were repealed, but planning conditions, client requirements and BREEAM often still ask for one. The position can differ in Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland, so check what applies to your site.

### What is waste duty of care in construction?
Under the Environmental Protection Act 1990, anyone who produces or handles waste must take reasonable steps to store it safely, pass it only to someone authorised to take it, and describe it accurately on a transfer note. Check carrier registrations before the first skip leaves, not after.

### Who is responsible for waste on a construction site?
Everyone producing waste has a duty of care, but the principal contractor normally coordinates it and should name someone to run the plan day to day. Make sure subcontractors know whether they remove their own waste or use the site skips, because that changes who signs the transfer notes.

### What records do I need to keep for construction waste?
Waste transfer notes for non-hazardous waste, consignment notes for hazardous waste, and copies of carrier registrations and site permits. File them against the plan so you can show where every load went.

### Can AI write a site waste management plan?
It can draft the structure and procedures from your project details. The carriers, sites, quantities, segregation and targets have to be real, checked and owned by the site team. A plan full of generic text and made-up tonnages won't stand up to a client audit.
