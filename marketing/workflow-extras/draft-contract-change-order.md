## When not to use it
Don't use it to create a change that hasn't been properly instructed or agreed. A well-formatted document from someone without the authority to instruct doesn't make it a valid variation, and it can muddy your position later.

If the scope or value is still being argued about, you're not ready for a change order. Settle it through the contract's own process first. Check your contract and take advice. And if your contract runs instructions and compensation events through an online system, issue it there. A separate Word document can end up as a second, conflicting record.

## Common mistakes
- Trusting the clause numbers the tool puts in the contractual basis section. It will often cite the wrong clause or the wrong edition. Check every reference against your actual contract and its amendments.
- Calling it a change order when your contract needs an instruction from a named person.
- Vague scope, like "additional works as discussed". Name the areas, drawings, revisions and specification references.
- Agreeing the cost and leaving time as "to be confirmed". Deal with time and money together.
- Forgetting to pass the change down to the subcontractors affected, on matching terms.

## FAQs
### What is a change order in UK construction?
It's the American term for what UK contracts usually call a variation. Under JCT it's an instruction from the architect, contract administrator or employer. Under NEC4 it's a Project Manager's instruction that becomes a compensation event. Use your contract's terms in the document.

### Who can sign a change order under JCT or NEC4?
Only the person the contract gives that power to. That's usually the architect or contract administrator on JCT traditional forms, the employer or their agent on JCT design and build, and the Project Manager under NEC4. AI can't decide who has authority. Check your contract and take advice.

### What happens if a variation is only instructed verbally?
Most contracts expect instructions in writing, and some set out a process for confirming oral ones. Confirm it in writing straight away, with the date, who gave it and exactly what was asked for. Check your contract and take advice, because the rules and time limits vary.

### Should a change order include the effect on time?
Yes. Say which activities are affected and whether the completion date moves, even if the answer is no. Leaving time out can make it harder to recover later. Check your contract and take advice.
