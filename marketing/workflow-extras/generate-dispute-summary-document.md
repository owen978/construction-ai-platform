## When not to use it
Don't use it as your adjudication submission or as legal advice. It organises what you already know into a summary for internal review or for briefing your advisers. The referral, the claim and any view on the merits need review by someone who knows construction law. Check your contract and take advice.

It's also the wrong tool if you haven't gathered the documents. A summary built from memory and a long email chain will read convincingly and still be wrong. Check with your solicitor before putting their advice or other privileged material into any AI tool, and only ever use a business account with data controls switched on.

## Common mistakes
- Writing the chronology from memory instead of from dated documents. Every fact should point to a document.
- Writing your own position as fact and the other side's as opinion. A one-sided summary won't help you judge the risk.
- Letting the tool fill in clause numbers or legal principles. Check every one against the contract, and have anything legal reviewed.
- Losing track of which notices went in and when. Many disputes turn on whether the right notice was given on time.

## FAQs
### Can AI tell me if I'll win an adjudication?
No. It can lay out the arguments and show gaps in the evidence, but the merits and likely outcome are a judgement for experienced people who know the contract and the law. Treat any outcome range in the draft as a starting point for that discussion. Check your contract and take advice.

### What's the difference between adjudication and mediation?
Adjudication is a statutory right under the Construction Act for most construction contracts, and the decision is binding until the dispute is finally decided by a court, arbitration or agreement. Mediation is voluntary: a neutral mediator helps the parties reach their own settlement, and nothing is imposed. Check your contract and take advice on which route suits your dispute.

### What evidence do I need for a construction dispute?
The contract documents, instructions, notices, correspondence, minutes, programmes, site diaries, photos and valuations, all dated. Records made at the time carry far more weight than anything written after the dispute started. An evidence matrix linking each document to each issue shows up the gaps early.

### When should I get a solicitor or claims consultant involved?
Early, and certainly before you issue anything formal such as a notice of adjudication. Getting advice while your position is still forming is usually easier than repairing a weak one later. Check your contract and take advice, especially on any steps or time limits in the dispute resolution clause.
