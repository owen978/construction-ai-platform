## When not to use it
Don't use it as your construction phase plan. Under CDM 2015 the principal contractor has to prepare one before the construction phase starts, and that needs a competent person who knows the site. The PEP can point to it, but it isn't it.

It's also the wrong tool for the Building Safety Act side of higher-risk building work, where the gateway submissions and competence requirements need specialist input. And if you don't have the contract documents yet, the scope and design responsibility sections will be guesswork.

## Common mistakes
- Issuing the draft as written. If a paragraph could apply to any job, it isn't doing anything.
- A scope section that doesn't match the contract, especially the exclusions and, on design and build, the employer's requirements and contractor's proposals.
- A design responsibility matrix nobody has agreed with the designers and specialist subcontractors.
- Long-lead items listed without real lead times from the supply chain.
- Writing it once at the start and never updating it as the team, programme or procurement changes.

## FAQs
### What is a project execution plan in construction?
A document setting out how the project will be delivered: the team, and how design, procurement, programme, cost, risk, safety and quality will be managed. It's the reference point the team works to. A good one is specific to the job, not a company manual with the project name changed.

### Who writes the project execution plan, the client or the contractor?
Both can. On many UK projects the client's project manager writes a PEP for the whole project, and the contractor writes its own plan for delivering the works. Be clear whose document you're writing, and make sure the two line up.

### Is a project execution plan a legal requirement?
Not in itself. The construction phase plan is a legal requirement under CDM 2015, and on higher-risk buildings the Building Safety Act brings its own duties. A PEP is good practice, and some clients make it a contract requirement, so check what yours asks for.

### What is the difference between a PEP and a construction phase plan?
The PEP covers how the whole project is run: scope, team, design, procurement, commercial and reporting. The construction phase plan is the CDM 2015 document setting out how health and safety will be managed during construction. They should be consistent, but one can't stand in for the other.

### How often should a PEP be updated?
At each major stage, such as contract award, start on site and the move into fit-out, and whenever the team, programme or procurement route changes. Give it a revision number and a named owner. Otherwise nobody knows which version is current.
