## When not to use it
Don't use it before you've read the job. The workflow gives you a solid structure, but the items that matter come from this contract, this site and this team: the access date, the consents still outstanding, the design that isn't finished. Go through the contract documents and the pre-construction information first.

It's also the wrong tool for a meeting that's really a negotiation. If scope, price or programme are still being argued over, hold a separate commercial meeting with the right people in the room.

## Common mistakes
- Reusing the last job's agenda, so this site's constraints, such as a neighbouring school or live services, never get discussed.
- Giving every item equal time. Spend it where the risk is: outstanding design information, long-lead orders, access and third-party consents.
- Not confirming the CDM 2015 duty holders by name. In England, the building regulations principal designer and principal contractor need naming too, and they aren't always the same firms.
- Issuing the agenda the day before, so nobody brings the information you need.
- Recording actions without a named owner and a date.

## FAQs
### What should be on a pre-construction meeting agenda?
Introductions and roles, the contract and key dates, design responsibility and outstanding information, programme and long-lead items, procurement, health and safety, quality, logistics, neighbours and consents, document control, the meeting schedule and actions. Weight each item by where the risk sits on this job.

### Who should attend a pre-start meeting?
The client's project manager and cost consultant, the design team and the principal designer, plus the contractor's project manager, site manager, design manager and QS. Subcontractors with design responsibility or long-lead items are worth inviting too. Make sure someone at the table can make decisions for the client.

### Is a pre-construction meeting a contractual requirement?
The standard forms don't always call for one, but the employer's requirements or the Scope often set out a meeting structure. Either way, the minutes don't change the contract on their own. If the meeting agrees something that departs from the contract, such as a different access date, deal with it through the contract. Check your contract and take advice.

### Can AI take the minutes as well?
It can turn your notes or a transcript into tidy minutes and an action table, but someone who was in the room must check them before they go out. Get everyone's agreement before recording a meeting, and use a business account with data controls switched on, not a personal one. The chair owns the record, not the tool.
