## When not to use it
Don't use it as the inspection. It gives you a checklist to walk the building with; the snags come from what you find. Whether a defect is acceptable is the site manager's call against the specification, and completion is for the certifier.

It's not the right tool for fire safety or building regulations sign-off either. Fire stopping, compartmentation and fire doors need checking by competent people, and higher-risk buildings have their own completion process under the Building Safety Act. A template can remind you to look. It can't sign anything off.

## Common mistakes
- Leaving all the snagging to one inspection at the end. Get each trade to snag its own work as areas finish, so your inspection finds less.
- Raising snags with no responsible trade against them, so they sit on the list while each subcontractor blames the next.
- Inspecting before the clean or in poor light, so marks get missed.
- Not agreeing the standard first. Settle the benchmarks, such as sample panels or the NHBC Standards on new homes, before the walk round, not during the argument.
- Forgetting the areas nobody lives in: risers, plant rooms, the roof and the external works.

## FAQs
### What is snagging in construction?
Checking finished work for defects, incomplete items and damage, and getting them put right before handover. It usually happens in the run-up to practical completion. Defects that show up later are dealt with under the contract's defects arrangements.

### What's the difference between snags and defects?
Snags are usually minor items picked up before or at handover, such as damaged paintwork or a sticking door. Defects is the wider contractual term for work that doesn't meet the contract, and they can appear after completion: under JCT they're dealt with in the rectification period, and under NEC4 up to the defects date. Check your contract and take advice.

### Can practical completion be certified with snags outstanding?
Often, if the items are minor and don't stop the building being used as intended, but it depends on the contract and the facts. JCT doesn't define practical completion, while NEC4 defines Completion by reference to the Scope. The decision sits with the certifier the contract names, not the contractor and not an AI tool. Check your contract and take advice.

### Who should carry out a snagging inspection?
The contractor's site manager should snag first, with each trade signing off its own work. The client's side, often an employer's agent or clerk of works, then carries out its own inspection. On new homes, buyers sometimes bring in an independent snagging inspector as well.
