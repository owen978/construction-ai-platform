## When not to use it
Don't use it to decide who's responsible for the delay. If you think some or all of it isn't yours, a recovery programme can look like you're accepting it, or offering to accelerate at your own cost. That's an entitlement question. Check your contract and take advice before anything goes to the client.

It also won't produce the programme. The narrative has to describe a real, logic-linked recovery programme built by a planner in Asta or Primavera. And it's not a delay analysis. If the delay is heading for a claim or adjudication, you need a planning or delay specialist.

## Common mistakes
- Claiming time savings the programme doesn't show. A second steel gang doesn't help if the crane, access or design information is the real constraint.
- Adding Saturday working without checking planning conditions, permitted hours or noise consents.
- Changing the sequence without the temporary works coordinator's sign-off, for example starting cladding off an independent scaffold before the frame is finished. AI can't approve temporary works.
- Putting more trades into the same area without updating the construction phase plan and site logistics.
- Describing the causes of delay loosely. That section may be read later in a claim, so keep it factual and consistent with your notices.

## FAQs
### What is a recovery programme in construction?
A revised programme showing how a project that has fallen behind will get back to a target completion date. It should show the changed sequence, the extra resources and the new critical path, against the original baseline so the changes are clear. The narrative explains it for people who won't read the Gantt chart.

### Who pays for acceleration on a construction project?
It depends on who caused the delay and what your contract says. If the delay is yours, recovering it is usually at your own cost. If it's the client's, acceleration normally needs to be instructed or agreed, with the price agreed first. Check your contract and take advice.

### Does submitting a recovery programme mean accepting the delay?
Not automatically, but the wording matters. If you believe you're entitled to an extension of time, say in the covering letter that you're reserving your position, and keep your delay notices up to date. Check your contract and take advice.

### What is the difference between mitigation and acceleration?
Mitigation is taking reasonable steps to reduce the effect of a delay. JCT contracts, for example, expect you to use your best endeavours to prevent delay. Acceleration goes further: extra resources or hours to finish earlier than you otherwise would, usually at extra cost. Where that line sits decides who pays, so check your contract and take advice.
