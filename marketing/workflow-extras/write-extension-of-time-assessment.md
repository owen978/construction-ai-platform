## When not to use it
Don't use it to decide entitlement. AI drafts and organises the analysis and the narrative. The contract administrator, project manager, QS or legal adviser makes the call.

On its own, it's also the wrong tool for a large or disputed delay claim. That needs a programme analysis by someone who can defend it. Check your contract and take advice.

## Common mistakes
- Using the wrong programme. Assess each event against the accepted programme current at the time, updated for progress.
- Ignoring notice compliance. A strong event can fail on a missed notice, especially under NEC4 or an amended JCT form.
- Adding up the days claimed for each event when the events overlap, which double counts the delay.
- Skipping concurrency, or assuming there's no contractor delay in the period. The assessor will look for it.
- Letting the tool fill gaps. If a date isn't in your records, it shouldn't be in the assessment.

## FAQs
### What is an extension of time in construction?
More time to complete the works, because of an event the contract puts at the employer's risk, such as a late instruction or a change. It moves the completion date, which protects the contractor from damages for that period. It doesn't automatically bring extra money. Check your contract and take advice on what each event entitles you to.

### How do you calculate an extension of time?
Identify the event, show which activities it affected, and test whether it delayed completion through the critical path or was absorbed by float. The SCL Delay and Disruption Protocol describes several delay analysis methods and suggests choosing one that suits the records and the timing. Check your contract and take advice on what the contract expects.

### What is concurrent delay in construction?
Delay to completion caused by an employer risk event and a contractor risk event at the same time. In England and Wales the usual approach is time but no money for the concurrent period, but contracts are often amended on this point. Check your contract and take advice.

### Who owns float in a construction programme?
It depends on the contract. The SCL Protocol's view is that, unless the contract says otherwise, an extension is only due once the delay uses up the float on the affected path. NEC4 protects the contractor's terminal float between planned completion and the completion date. Check your contract and take advice.

### Is the SCL Delay and Disruption Protocol binding?
Not unless your contract brings it in. It's guidance, but it's widely used as a reference for good practice in UK delay disputes. Following it makes an assessment easier to defend.
