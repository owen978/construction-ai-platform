## When not to use it
Don't treat a chat reply as your cash flow model. Use the workflow to organise the inputs and explain the shape of the forecast, then keep the calculation in a spreadsheet where every figure can be checked.

It's also not a substitute for the business's own cash forecast across all jobs, which needs real bank, payroll and overhead figures.

## Common mistakes
- Spreading value evenly across the programme instead of following the actual sequence of work.
- Ignoring payment terms: the gap between doing the work, the application, the payment notice and the money arriving.
- Forgetting retention, both what's held from you and what you hold from subcontractors.
- Leaving out large one-off costs such as plant purchases, bonds or materials paid up front.
- Not updating the forecast when the programme changes.

## FAQs
### What is an S-curve in construction?
A chart of cumulative cost or value against time. It's usually shaped like an S because spending starts slowly, speeds up through the main works and slows towards completion.

### Can AI build the forecast for me?
It can turn your programme, values and payment terms into a structured monthly profile and explain it. Keep the numbers in a spreadsheet you control and check them.

### How do payment terms change the forecast?
They shift when money comes in. A month of work might not be paid until weeks later, so the cash position lags the value earned.

### How often should I update it?
At least monthly, alongside the valuation and the CVR, and whenever the programme changes significantly.
