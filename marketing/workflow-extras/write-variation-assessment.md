## When not to use it
Don't use it before you know the change has been properly instructed. An assessment of an unauthorised change gives it weight it hasn't earned.

It's also the wrong tool for disputed entitlement on a large claim. There you need the contract, the records and usually specialist advice, not a structured draft.

## Common mistakes
- Valuing the change without checking which contract rules apply (for example JCT valuation rules or NEC4 compensation event procedures).
- Using rates the tool suggests instead of contract rates or properly built-up star rates.
- Leaving out the time effect. A variation that delays the work needs its programme impact assessed and notified.
- Missing the knock-on effects: preliminaries, design, temporary works and other trades.
- Sending the assessment without the supporting records that justify the quantities.

## FAQs
### Can AI tell me whether a variation is valid?
No. It can help you lay out the instruction, scope and valuation, but whether a change is a valid variation depends on your contract and the facts. Check your contract and take advice where it matters.

### What rates should the assessment use?
Contract rates where they apply, rates based on contract rates where the work is similar, and properly built-up rates where it isn't. Give the tool your rates; don't let it invent them.

### Does this work for NEC4 compensation events?
The structure helps, but NEC4 has its own process and time limits for notifying and quoting compensation events. Use the compensation event workflow and follow your contract.

### How do I show the time effect?
Say which activities are affected, whether they're on the critical path and what that does to the completion date, backed by your programme. If there's a delay, make sure the right notice goes in on time.
