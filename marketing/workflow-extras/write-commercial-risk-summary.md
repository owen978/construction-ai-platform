## When not to use it
Don't use it to set your forecast. It reports risks and opportunities you've already valued, and the numbers should come from an up-to-date CVR.

It's also not the place to decide claims strategy. Where a risk depends on a disputed compensation event, a claim or a contra charge, the summary should state the position and the exposure, not argue the merits. AI drafts and organises. The commercial manager and the directors make the call.

## Common mistakes
- Double counting: listing a risk that's already allowed for in the CVR forecast.
- Being optimistic about opportunities, which makes the net position look better than it is.
- Leaving risks in the register after they've happened. By then they're issues, and the cost belongs in the forecast.
- Letting the tool do the sums. Check the gross and weighted figures, and that the adjusted margin ties back to the CVR.
- Risks with no owner and no date for the next action, so the same list comes back every month unchanged.

## FAQs
### What should a commercial risk summary include?
A short statement of the overall position, risk and opportunity registers with value, likelihood, owner and action, the net exposure, and the effect on forecast margin. Add a watchlist for items you can't value yet. Keep it to what the board needs to decide.

### How do you calculate weighted risk exposure?
Multiply the gross exposure by the likelihood, expressed as a percentage. It gives a figure you can total and compare with your risk allowance, but it's an average, not a prediction. Show the gross figures too, because one large risk can still land in full.

### What is the difference between a risk and an issue in construction?
A risk is something that might happen. An issue is something that has happened and now needs managing. Move issues out of the risk register and into the cost forecast.

### How should disputed compensation events be shown in a risk report?
Show the value you've submitted, the value the Project Manager has assessed or is likely to assess, and the gap as the exposure. Say what's being done to resolve it and by when. Check your contract and take advice before relying on a disputed sum in the forecast.

### Does NEC4 Option C change how commercial risk is reported?
Yes. Under a target cost contract, overruns and savings are shared through the pain/gain share, so the contractor's exposure isn't always the full amount. Disallowed Cost is different, because it isn't paid at all. Check your contract and take advice on how the share ranges apply.
