## When not to use it
Don't use it to decide what you're owed. The workflow organises your measure, your percentages and your records into a clear application. It can't measure the work, and it can't tell you whether an item is properly instructed.

Skip it on very small packages with three or four lines, where typing the application by hand is quicker than preparing the inputs.

## Common mistakes
- Pasting in last month's application and letting old quantities carry forward without re-measuring.
- Including variations that haven't been instructed yet as if they were agreed. Show them separately or leave them out.
- Forgetting retention, previous payments or materials on site, so the amount due doesn't match the cumulative value.
- Missing the payment notice and pay less notice dates. The application is only half the job; the dates decide when money moves.
- Relying on the tool's arithmetic. Check every total against your spreadsheet.

## FAQs
### Can AI work out my valuation for me?
No. It can lay out the valuation, check the arithmetic and flag lines that look inconsistent, but the quantities and percentages have to come from your own measure and site records.

### Will the application meet the Construction Act?
The workflow helps you present it clearly, but the payment dates and notices are set by your contract, or by the Scheme for Construction Contracts if the contract doesn't cover them. Check your contract and take advice if you're unsure.

### How should I show variations that aren't agreed yet?
List them separately from the agreed account, with the instruction reference and your valuation, so the client can see what's claimed and what's still being discussed.

### Is it safe to paste a valuation into an AI tool?
Use a business account with data controls switched on, and remove anything you're not allowed to share, such as client names or confidential rates.
