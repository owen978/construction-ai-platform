## When not to use it
Don't use it to work out your hazards or controls. The induction has to reflect the construction phase plan, the risk assessments and what's actually happening on site this week. The site manager, for the principal contractor, owns the content and the delivery, not the tool.

It also won't replace task-specific training or RAMS briefings. A general induction doesn't make anyone competent to work in a confined space, drive a telehandler or work near asbestos. If the site has a specialist hazard, such as licensed asbestos removal, get the specialist contractor or your H&S adviser to check that section.

## Common mistakes
- Using a generic company induction that doesn't mention this site's access routes, assembly point or hazards.
- Not updating it when the phase changes. A groundworks induction isn't right for fit-out, and a new crane, scaffold change or road closure needs briefing to everyone, not just new starters.
- Reading it out without checking anyone understood. Ask questions, use the site plan, and make sure workers with limited English get it in a form they can follow.
- Forgetting visitors and delivery drivers, who need a shorter briefing but still need the rules and the emergency arrangements.

## FAQs
### What should a site induction cover?
Who's who, the site rules and PPE, emergency arrangements and first aid, how to report accidents and near misses, this site's hazards and their controls, permits to work, welfare and who to raise concerns with. The point under CDM 2015 is information about this site, not a general safety talk.

### Who is responsible for site inductions under CDM 2015?
The principal contractor must make sure a suitable site induction is provided, and on a job with only one contractor, that contractor takes on the duty. Contractors must also make sure their own workers are inducted where the principal contractor hasn't done it. AI can draft the content, but deciding who is competent and fit to start work is the site team's job.

### Do visitors need a site induction?
They need a briefing, usually shorter than the full induction. Visitors need the site rules, PPE, emergency arrangements and who is escorting them, and they shouldn't go into work areas alone. Delivery drivers need the traffic route, the unloading area and the banksman arrangements.

### Does a site induction need to be signed?
Understanding matters more than the signature, but a signed and dated record shows who was inducted, when and on which version. Keep it with your site records, and re-induct people when the content changes significantly.
