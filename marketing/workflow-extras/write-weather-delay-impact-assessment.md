## When not to use it
Don't use it to decide whether the weather was bad enough to count. That's a judgement on the records against the contract test, made by the contract administrator or project manager. AI drafts and organises. It doesn't make that call.

Be careful on amended contracts too, where weather is sometimes moved entirely to the contractor. Check your contract and take advice before relying on weather for time.

## Common mistakes
- Measuring the weather against your programme allowance instead of the contract test. Under NEC4 the comparison is with the weather data in the contract.
- Claiming every bad day. Only days when the weather actually stopped critical work count, and normal winter weather is your risk.
- Using records from a distant weather station, or a different one from the place named in the contract.
- Counting weekends or other non-working days as days lost.
- Writing it up weeks later from memory. The site diary should record each day's conditions and what stopped.

## FAQs
### Is bad weather a relevant event under JCT?
Exceptionally adverse weather is a Relevant Event under the standard JCT forms, so it can give an extension of time. The word "exceptionally" matters: weather that's normal for the site and the time of year is the contractor's risk. Check your contract and take advice.

### Can you claim money for weather delays?
Under JCT, usually not: weather gives time but no loss and expense. Under NEC4, weather that passes the compensation event test is assessed for time and money, but only the difference between the actual weather and the one in ten year level counts. Check your contract and take advice.

### How does the NEC4 weather compensation event work?
Weather measured over a calendar month, at the place named in the Contract Data, is compared with the weather data for that month. If it occurs on average less than once in ten years, it's a compensation event. Check your contract and take advice.

### Where do I get weather data for a delay claim?
Use a recognised source, usually the Met Office, for the station named in the contract, or the nearest suitable one if none is named. Match it to your site records: daily diaries, photos and notes of which activities stopped and why. The data shows the weather. Your records show the delay.

### Do I need to give notice of a weather delay?
Yes. JCT asks for notice when it becomes reasonably apparent that progress is being or is likely to be delayed, and NEC4 requires compensation events to be notified within a time limit. Late notice can weaken or lose the claim. Check your contract and take advice.
