## When not to use it
Don't run it until the numbers behind it are settled. If the cost value reconciliation or the programme update isn't done, the summary just makes unchecked figures look board-ready.

It's also the wrong tool for a project in real trouble. If the board has to decide something big, like taking a loss or replacing a failing subcontractor, give them the full paper with the evidence, not 200 words. And it's an internal report. Don't send it to the client as it stands.

## Common mistakes
- Using RAG colours with no agreed definitions. Set the triggers once for the whole portfolio.
- Reporting cost to date against budget instead of the forecast final margin.
- Counting unagreed variations and claims as secured income. Show them separately, as risk or opportunity.
- Green on the outside, red underneath. If the commercial or programme position is slipping, the colour has to say so.
- Asking for a vague "steer" instead of a clear decision with an owner and a date.

## FAQs
### What should an executive summary for a construction project include?
The overall status, where the programme and money stand against the last report, the one or two risks that matter and what's being done about them, and any decision the board needs to make. Lead with the headline or the decision, not the project history. Most board members read the first paragraph and skim the rest.

### How long should a project executive summary be?
One page at most. If you're reporting a portfolio, keep every project to the same format and length so the board can compare them. The detail belongs in the full monthly report.

### What does a RAG rating mean in construction reporting?
Red, amber, green: a quick signal of whether a project is on track, at risk or in trouble. It only works if the business agrees what each colour means, for example how far behind programme or how much margin slippage turns a project amber. Without those rules, the colours just reflect how optimistic each project manager is.

### How do I report bad news to the board?
Early and plainly. Say what's happened, what it does to the programme and margin, what you're doing about it and what you need from them. Boards can deal with problems. What they don't like is finding out late.

### Is it safe to put project figures into an AI tool?
Use a business account your company has approved, with data controls switched on. Don't paste client names, margins or commercially sensitive figures into a personal account. If in doubt, anonymise the project before you run the prompt.
