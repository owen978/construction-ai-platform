## When not to use it
Don't use it in place of an accident investigation. A RIDDOR incident or a serious near miss needs its own investigation by competent people, and the lessons learned report should only refer to the outcome.

Be careful if the project is in dispute or heading that way. A frank internal report about what went wrong could be disclosed if the dispute goes formal. Check your contract and take advice before you write it, and keep the facts separate from opinion.

## Common mistakes
- Writing it from the project manager's notes alone. Get the site team, the QS, the designers and the key subcontractors involved, even if it's just a call.
- Lessons too vague to act on, like "communication could be better". Say what happened, why, and what to do differently next time.
- Recommendations with no owner or date, so nothing changes.
- Naming and blaming individuals or subcontractors in a document that gets circulated. Focus on the process, not the person.
- Filing it and never feeding it back into estimating, tendering and the next project's execution plan.

## FAQs
### When should lessons learned be captured on a construction project?
Throughout the job, not just at the end. Capture them at key stages such as the end of design, frame completion, a watertight envelope and handover, while people still remember the detail. A short review at each stage makes the final report far easier to write.

### How do you run a lessons learned session?
Get the right people together, set a clear agenda and keep it about the process, not individuals. For each area, ask what went well, what didn't and what you'd do differently. Agree the actions and owners before anyone leaves the room.

### How do you write a good lessons learned statement?
Make it specific and actionable. State what happened, the effect it had, the underlying cause and the change you recommend, with an owner. "Appoint the M&E subcontractor in time to coordinate the design before the frame goes up" is far more useful than "procure earlier".

### What is the difference between a lessons learned report and a closeout report?
The closeout report records how the project performed on programme, cost, quality and safety. The lessons learned report explains why, and what the business will do differently. The closeout report usually summarises the main lessons and points to the full report.

### Can AI write a lessons learned report?
It can organise raw notes into a clear structure and suggest root causes worth discussing. It can't tell you which lessons matter most to your business. Take names out of the notes first, and use a business account your company has approved.
