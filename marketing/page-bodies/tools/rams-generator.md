## What does an AI-generated RAMS include?

Each draft covers the sections a principal contractor expects to see:

- **Scope of works:** what the task is and where it happens.
- **Risk assessment:** each hazard, who is at risk, a likelihood and severity score before and after controls, and the control measures.
- **Method statement:** a step-by-step sequence from set-up to clear-up.
- **PPE:** what everyone doing the task needs to wear.
- **Competence:** the training, cards and tickets the task needs.
- **Emergency arrangements:** first aid, emergency contacts and what to do if something goes wrong.

## What does a sample output look like?

**Sample output: illustrative only, not for use on site without review**

**Task:** installing internal metal stud and plasterboard partitions on an upper floor of an occupied-building refurbishment, using podium steps and a mobile access tower for the head track.

**Scope:** set out, fix head and floor track, install studs at the specified centres, fit insulation, board both sides with plasterboard, tape and fill. Services in the zone are isolated or confirmed clear before fixing.

**Risk assessment (likelihood x severity, each scored 1 to 5):**

| Hazard | Who's at risk | Before controls | Controls | After controls |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Falls from podium steps or mobile tower when fixing head track | Operatives | 3 x 4 = 12 | Tower erected by PASMA-trained person and inspected before use, guardrails and toe boards fitted, brakes on, no overreaching, podium steps with gates closed | 1 x 4 = 4 |
| Manual handling of full plasterboard sheets | Operatives | 4 x 3 = 12 | Two-person lift for full sheets, board trolley from the hoist to the work area, boards stored flat near the point of use | 2 x 3 = 6 |
| Dust from cutting and sanding board and filler | Operatives, others nearby | 3 x 3 = 9 | Score and snap rather than power cutting, on-tool extraction for any sanding, FFP3 masks where extraction can't be used, area screened off | 1 x 3 = 3 |
| Striking hidden services when fixing to the slab or soffit | Operatives, building users | 2 x 5 = 10 | Check the services drawings, scan before drilling, permit for any work near live services | 1 x 5 = 5 |
| Cuts from metal stud and track | Operatives | 3 x 2 = 6 | Cut-resistant gloves, snips kept sharp, offcuts binned as you go | 2 x 2 = 4 |
| Slips and trips from offcuts and cables | Everyone on the floor | 3 x 2 = 6 | Clear as you go, cables routed at height or along walls, daily housekeeping check | 1 x 2 = 2 |

**Method statement (first steps):**

1. Supervisor briefs the gang on this RAMS and records attendance before work starts.
2. Confirm the services in the partition zone are isolated or marked, and the floor is clear.
3. Erect and inspect the mobile tower, record the inspection, and set out the partition lines.
4. Fix the floor track, then the head track from the tower, working within the guardrails.

**PPE:** hard hat, safety boots, hi-vis, cut-resistant gloves, safety glasses, FFP3 mask when cutting or sanding without extraction.

**Sign-off:** this draft must be checked against the actual site, the principal contractor's rules and the construction phase plan, then signed off by a competent person before use.

Your output will reflect the task, site and equipment you describe.

## Is an AI-generated RAMS good enough to use on site?

Not as it comes out. It's a first draft, and the duty to make it right for the job stays with the people doing the work. Used properly, it gets the structure and the standard controls down quickly so the time goes on the parts that matter.

Before it's used, someone competent in the task, meaning they have the right training, knowledge and experience for this work, needs to check it against the actual site. That means the real access and working areas, the plant you're actually bringing, other trades working nearby, the principal contractor's site rules and the construction phase plan. Change anything that doesn't match, add what's missing, then sign it off and brief it to everyone doing the work.

> **QS take:** Most RAMS I've seen rejected weren't wrong. They were generic: the same sheet with the task name changed. Use the draft to get the structure and the standard controls down quickly, then spend the time you've saved on the bits that are specific to this job and this site. That's what the person reviewing it is actually looking for.

## What does AI miss in a RAMS?

It only knows what you tell it, so the gaps are usually the things that make your site different:

- **Site-specific hazards:** an occupied building, a busy public footpath, an asbestos register entry, a weak floor.
- **Sequencing clashes with other trades:** who else is working on the floor, above you or below you at the same time.
- **Emergency routes:** the actual escape routes, assembly point and first aiders on this site, not generic ones.
- **The client's and principal contractor's own rules:** permits, hours, exclusion zones and induction requirements.

Read the draft with these in mind and add them before sign-off.

### Is an AI-generated RAMS legally valid?

An AI-generated RAMS is a first draft, not a finished document. It must be reviewed, tailored to the actual site, and signed off by a competent person before use. Used that way it is just as valid as one written from scratch, and far quicker to produce. What makes a RAMS valid is the review and sign-off, not who typed it.
