## When to use it
When a public sector opportunity lands from the Find a Tender service or a buyer's portal and you need to decide quickly whether it is worth pursuing. Notices under the Procurement Act 2023 can be long, and the details that matter (the procedure, the conditions of participation, the lots and the deadlines) are spread across several sections. This gets them onto one page so the bid or no-bid conversation starts from the facts. The notice and the tender documents always win over the summary.

## Prompt
You are an experienced UK construction estimator who bids for public sector work. Below is a tender notice published under the Procurement Act 2023, plus any linked documents I have downloaded.

Our business: [SIZE AND TYPE OF CONTRACTOR, e.g. a regional main contractor doing education and healthcare work up to a set value]
What we usually bid for: [TYPES OF WORK, LOCATIONS AND CONTRACT SIZES]

Summarise the notice as a one-page brief with these headings:
1. The buyer and what they want built or delivered, in plain words.
2. Estimated value, contract length and any options to extend, exactly as stated.
3. Procedure (for example open or competitive flexible), any stages, and whether it is a framework or has lots.
4. Key dates: clarification deadline, submission deadline, any site visits or briefings, and expected award and start dates.
5. Conditions of participation: turnover, insurance, accreditations, experience and anything else we must meet to take part.
6. Award criteria and weightings, including quality, price and social value if given.
7. The contract form and any unusual terms mentioned.
8. A short "fit with our business" note comparing the notice with what I told you about us.
9. Questions to raise with the buyer through the clarification process.

Rules: quote figures and dates exactly as they appear in the notice. Do not estimate a value or assume a date. If something is not in what I have pasted, write "Not stated in the notice" and list it under a heading "Still to find". Use UK spelling.

Tender notice and documents:
[PASTE NOTICE TEXT AND ANY LINKED DOCUMENTS]

## What it produced
A one-page brief under nine headings, with the value, dates and conditions lifted straight from the notice, a short fit note against your own business profile, a list of items the notice doesn't state, and draft clarification questions ready to put through the buyer's portal.

## What you still check
- Every date and deadline against the live notice and the portal, as buyers can change them through later notices or clarifications.
- That you meet each condition of participation on paper, with evidence you can actually provide.
- The award criteria and weightings against the full tender documents, not just the notice.
- That anything commercially sensitive about your business stays inside your company's approved AI tools.

## Steps
1. Download the notice and any linked documents from the Find a Tender service or the buyer's portal.
2. Run the prompt with a short profile of your business and check the brief against the notice.
3. Take the brief to your bid or no-bid meeting and send any clarification questions before the deadline.
