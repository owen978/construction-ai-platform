## When to use it
As soon as it becomes reasonably apparent that progress is, or is likely to be, delayed. Under JCT Design and Build the contractor has to give notice of the cause of delay, say which Relevant Event it believes applies, and follow up with particulars of the expected effects and an estimate of the delay to completion. Late or thin notices are one of the most common reasons good extension of time cases fail. This drafts the notice and the application from your own programme and records so nothing gets missed. It doesn't decide whether you're entitled. This is a high risk notice, so advice is strongly recommended, especially where liquidated damages are in play.

## Prompt
You are an experienced UK contracts manager drafting a notice of delay and an extension of time application under a JCT Design and Build Contract 2024 for a [SIZE AND TYPE OF PROJECT]. We are the Contractor.

Facts:
- Contract reference: [CONTRACT REFERENCE]
- Current Completion Date (or Section Completion Date): [DATE]
- The cause of delay, what happened and when we first knew: [DESCRIBE THE EVENT, WITH DATES]
- The Relevant Event we believe applies: [E.G. A CHANGE, AN EMPLOYER'S INSTRUCTION, EXCEPTIONALLY ADVERSE WEATHER]
- Programme activities affected and how: [PASTE ACTIVITIES, DURATIONS AND LOGIC]
- Our own estimate of delay to completion and how we worked it out: [ESTIMATE AND METHOD]
- What we have done, or are doing, to reduce the delay: [MITIGATION]
- Supporting records, with dates: [INSTRUCTIONS, SITE DIARY ENTRIES, EMAILS, WEATHER RECORDS, PHOTOS]

Draft two documents.
1. A short notice of delay: the material circumstances, the cause, the Relevant Event we rely on, and a statement that particulars and an estimate will follow if they aren't included yet. Under 250 words.
2. An extension of time application with these headings: Background, The event, Effect on the programme, Estimated delay to completion, Mitigation, Supporting records (as a table with Date, Record and What it shows), and Request.

Rules: use only the dates, figures and records I have given you. Do not calculate a new delay figure, invent an event or add a record. If the critical path link is unclear, a date is missing or the evidence is thin, put it in a list headed "Gaps before issue" instead of filling it in. Write factually, without blame. Do not quote clause numbers you are not sure of. Add a final line: "Check the contract terms and take advice before relying on this."

## What it produced
Two drafts. First, a short, neutral notice of delay that names the cause and the Relevant Event and leaves placeholders for the date, signatory and method of service. Second, a structured application that walks from the event through the programme effect to the estimate, with a mitigation section and a dated table of supporting records. Under both sits a "Gaps before issue" list of anything the facts didn't support, such as a missing date or an unclear link to the critical path. It ends with the reminder to check the contract.

## What you still check
- The notice goes out forthwith, through the method and to the address the contract requires. Don't hold the notice back while you finish the application.
- The Relevant Event you rely on is actually listed in your contract, including any amendments in the schedule of amendments.
- The delay estimate matches your own programme analysis, and the critical path link is shown, not just stated.
- Every record in the table exists, is dated correctly and says what the draft claims it says.
- Check the contract terms and take advice before relying on this. With extension of time, advice is strongly recommended.

## Steps
1. Gather the event dates, the affected programme activities, your own delay estimate and the dated records behind it.
2. Run the prompt in your company's approved AI tool, then clear every item on the "Gaps before issue" list.
3. Issue the notice straight away by the contract's method of service, then send the application and keep proof of when and how both were sent.
