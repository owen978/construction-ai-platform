## When to use it
The first week a hard frost is forecast, and again whenever the weather turns after a mild spell. Icy ladders, scaffold boards and haul roads catch people out first thing, before anything has thawed. Cold hands lose grip and feel, and people rush jobs to get back in the warm. The supervisor delivering the talk owns it and should adapt it to the site. AI only drafts it.

## Prompt
You are helping a UK site supervisor prepare a five-minute toolbox talk on working safely in cold, ice and frost.

About the site: [SIZE AND TYPE OF PROJECT, e.g. a four-storey frame with scaffold on three elevations]
Work happening this week: [e.g. brickwork, groundworks, roofing, deliveries]
Forecast: [e.g. overnight frost, ice on untreated surfaces until mid-morning]
Arrangements we have: [e.g. grit bins at the gate and scaffold base, who grits and when, heated welfare cabin, hot drinks, spare gloves in the stores]
Known problem areas: [e.g. north-facing scaffold lift, sloping path to the car park, standing water by the crane base]

Write a talk with:
- Why the cold matters on this site this week, in two or three sentences.
- The main risks, no more than five, in plain words. Think about slips on access routes, ice on scaffold, ladders and plant steps, frozen or sluggish plant, materials affected by frost, and cold hands and tiredness.
- What we do about each one, using only the arrangements I've listed. Where I haven't given an arrangement, write "[SITE ARRANGEMENT TO CONFIRM]" rather than inventing one.
- What to do if a route, scaffold or work area is icy, including stopping and who to tell.
- How to look after yourself and the people around you: layers, gloves that still let you grip, breaks in the warm, and spotting someone who is too cold.
- Three questions to ask the gang to check they've understood.

Rules: keep it under 400 words, short sentences, UK spelling, and no legal claims, temperature limits or statistics. Don't give rules for concrete, mortar or other materials in the cold; say "follow the specification and the manufacturer's guidance" instead. Refer to the site's RAMS and scaffold inspection arrangements rather than restating them.

## What it produced
A short talk the supervisor can read out in about five minutes: why the cold matters this week, up to five risks with the site's own arrangements against each, gaps marked as arrangements to confirm, a clear "if it's icy, stop and tell" instruction, a few lines on keeping warm and watching out for each other, and three check questions.

## What you still check
- Every arrangement in the talk is one the site actually has, and every "[SITE ARRANGEMENT TO CONFIRM]" gap is filled before you deliver it.
- Gritting is actually done before the first people arrive, and the problem areas named in the talk are treated, not just mentioned.
- Anything about materials in the cold matches the specification and the manufacturer's guidance, and the trades doing that work have been told.
- It lines up with the RAMS, scaffold inspections and any winter working arrangements your site already has.
- Everyone who attends signs the toolbox talk record.

## Steps
1. Walk the site first thing on a frosty morning and note the icy spots, access routes and the arrangements you have.
2. Run the prompt with those details and the forecast, then fill any gaps it marks.
3. Deliver it at the start of the shift, ask the three questions and get the record signed.
