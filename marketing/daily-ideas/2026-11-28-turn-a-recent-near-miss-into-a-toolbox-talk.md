## When to use it
Within a day or two of a near miss being reported, once the facts are clear and any immediate actions are in place. A talk about something that nearly happened on your own site lands far better than a generic one, and it shows people that reporting near misses leads to change. The supervisor delivering the talk owns it and should adapt it to the site. AI only drafts it. It is not an investigation, and it does not replace your company's near miss reporting process.

## Prompt
You are helping a UK site supervisor prepare a five-minute toolbox talk based on a near miss on their own site.

About the site: [SIZE AND TYPE OF PROJECT, e.g. a four-storey timber frame residential block]
What happened: [SHORT FACTUAL DESCRIPTION, with no names, e.g. a pallet of blocks slipped on a telehandler fork while being lifted near a walkway]
When and where: [e.g. Tuesday morning, rear of block B]
Why it could have been serious: [e.g. two operatives were on the walkway below]
What we have found so far: [KNOWN CAUSES, e.g. pallet damaged, exclusion zone not set out]
What changes now: [ACTIONS ALREADY AGREED, e.g. pallets checked before lifting, exclusion zone barriers for all lifts]

Write a talk with:
- What happened, in three or four plain sentences, with no names and no blame.
- Why it matters: what could have happened, without exaggerating.
- What we are changing, using only the actions I have listed. Where an action is still to be agreed, write "[ACTION TO CONFIRM]" rather than inventing one.
- A reminder of how to report a near miss on this site and why it helps.
- Three questions to ask the gang to check they have understood.

Rules: keep it under 400 words, short sentences, UK spelling, and no legal claims or statistics. Do not speculate about causes beyond what I have given you. Refer to the site's RAMS and lifting plan rather than restating them.

## What it produced
A short talk the supervisor can read out in about five minutes: a plain, blame-free account of the near miss, why it mattered, the changes the site has agreed with any gaps marked as actions to confirm, a reminder on reporting, and three check questions.

## What you still check
- The account is factual, names nobody and matches what the near miss report says.
- Every change in the talk has actually been agreed, and every "[ACTION TO CONFIRM]" gap is filled before you deliver it.
- The talk matches the RAMS, lifting plan or other documents it refers to, and those are updated if the near miss showed a gap.
- Everyone who attends signs the toolbox talk record.

## Steps
1. Gather the near miss report and the actions agreed so far, with names removed.
2. Run the prompt and fill any gaps it marks.
3. Deliver it at the start of the next shift, ask the three questions and get the record signed.
