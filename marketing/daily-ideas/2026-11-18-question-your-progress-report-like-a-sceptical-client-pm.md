## When to use it
The day before your monthly report goes to the client or employer's agent. You've written it, you've read it three times, and you can't see the holes any more. This gets a fresh pair of eyes on it that asks the awkward questions now, in private, rather than in the progress meeting with everyone watching.

## Prompt
You are an experienced client-side project manager on a UK construction project. You have seen a lot of contractor progress reports and you are hard to impress. You are reviewing the draft monthly progress report below for a [SIZE AND TYPE OF PROJECT], currently in month [X] of a [Y] month programme.

Read it as you would if it landed on your desk, and give me:
1. The ten questions you would most likely ask in the progress meeting, in order of how much they would worry you.
2. Any statement that sounds positive but isn't backed by a fact in the report (for example "on track", "recovering" or "minor slippage" with no dates or evidence). Quote the wording and say what evidence you would expect to see.
3. Anything that doesn't add up between sections, such as progress percentages that don't match the programme commentary, or risks that have disappeared since last month with no explanation.
4. Gaps: things a client PM would expect in a monthly report that are missing, such as key dates, change and early warning status, design information still outstanding, health and safety, and look-ahead.
5. Three suggested rewrites for the weakest sentences, using only facts already in the report.

Rules:
- Only work from what I have pasted. Do not invent dates, figures or events.
- If you need information I haven't given you to judge something, say so rather than guessing.
- Be direct, but keep it professional. Use UK spelling and plain English.

Last month's report (optional, for comparison):
[PASTE LAST MONTH'S REPORT]

Draft report:
[PASTE DRAFT REPORT]

## What it produced
A numbered list of likely client questions ranked by concern, a table of quoted claims next to the evidence a client would want for each, a short list of inconsistencies between sections or against last month, a checklist of missing headings, and a handful of tighter rewrites built only from the report's own facts.

## What you still check
- The questions are worth answering. Some will be things your client never asks about, so focus on the ones that match how they actually work.
- Any rewrite still says exactly what you mean and doesn't overstate or understate the position.
- Programme dates and progress figures against the live programme, not just the report text.
- Nothing commercially sensitive or internal has been added to the client version while you were tidying it up.

## Steps
1. Use a work AI account your company has approved, and paste the draft plus last month's report if you have it.
2. Work through the top questions and either answer them in the report or have the answer ready for the meeting.
3. Fix the unsupported claims with real dates and evidence, then send it.
