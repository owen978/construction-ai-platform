## When to use it
At the end of the day, once you've looked at tomorrow's look-ahead and know which trades are in, what's being delivered and which permits are live. It turns your notes into a briefing you can read out at the start of the shift, so the gangs hear the same message about where they're working, what's changed and who they'll be working around.

## Prompt
You are helping a UK site manager prepare a start-of-shift briefing for tomorrow on a [SIZE AND TYPE OF PROJECT].

Here is what I know about tomorrow:
- Short-term plan or look-ahead for tomorrow: [PASTE THE ACTIVITIES, AREAS AND TRADES]
- Deliveries and crane or hoist bookings: [PASTE TIMES, LOADS AND UNLOADING AREAS]
- Permits live tomorrow (hot works, excavation, working at height, other): [PASTE]
- Areas closed or restricted: [PASTE]
- Weather forecast: [PASTE]
- Anything else the team needs to know (visitors, inspections, changes to access routes): [PASTE]

Write a briefing I can read out in about five minutes. Use these headings:
1. Today in two sentences.
2. Who is working where: a short table with Area, Trade, Activity, Supervisor.
3. Where trades overlap: areas where two or more trades are working near each other or one above another, and what each needs to know.
4. Deliveries and lifts: times, routes and exclusion zones.
5. Permits and restricted areas.
6. Weather and what it means for today's work.
7. Anything that has changed since yesterday.
8. Two questions to ask the team to check they've understood.

Rules: only use what I've given you. Do not invent trades, times, permits or controls. Where something looks missing or clashes (for example a delivery at the same time as a lift in the same area), list it under a heading "Sort out before the briefing" at the top. Use short sentences, plain words and UK spelling.

## What it produced
A one-page briefing under the eight headings, with a small table of who is working where and a separate note of the areas where trades overlap. Above it, a short "Sort out before the briefing" list of gaps and clashes it spotted, such as a delivery with no unloading area or a permit with no named supervisor, ready for the site manager to fix before the shift starts.

## What you still check
- The plan matches what's really happening tomorrow, not what was planned last week.
- Every clash in the "Sort out before the briefing" list is resolved, and nothing has been quietly smoothed over.
- Permits, exclusion zones and access routes match the live permits and the site's own arrangements. The briefing doesn't replace the permit system or the RAMS.
- Names and supervisors are right, and anyone new to site still gets a full induction.

## Steps
1. At the end of the day, paste tomorrow's look-ahead, deliveries, permits and forecast into the prompt.
2. Fix everything in the "Sort out before the briefing" list, then print or save the briefing.
3. Read it out at the start of the shift, ask the two questions and update it if anything changes overnight.
